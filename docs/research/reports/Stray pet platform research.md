# Alerta Patinha wins by closing loops

Alerta Patinha should be a structured case layer, with tiered privacy, that plugs into the WhatsApp groups and the Facebook and Instagram networks where Brazilians already deal with lost, stray and injured animals. It should not be one more standalone app that has to win installs one neighborhood at a time. Three levers have the strongest evidence for getting people to use it. The first is a report that takes seconds: photo, location, send, with WhatsApp as a first-class intake channel. The second is closing the loop with photos that show every contributor what happened next, which is associated with a **57% higher probability of a second report** on FixMyStreet and produced **60% more requests** in Boston. The third is a personal ask to people nearby, with a claim that expires; the same mechanism raised bystander CPR from **48% to 62%** in a Stockholm trial. Points, leaderboards and streaks should stay out. The roughly 10% of contributors who do roughly 80% of the work are intrinsically motivated protetores, and the main threat to them is burnout from cases arriving with no limit. Trust and safety decides whether the platform helps or harms. Every abuse vector documented in Brazil feeds on a precise location, an exposed contact and an identifiable animal together: AI-edited "achei seu cachorro, manda Pix" scams, 234 look-alike Pix keys during the 2024 floods, chumbinho poisonings of cat colonies, theft of purebred dogs, and defamation suits over abuse accusations. The governing invariant is therefore that no public data lets a stranger find a home, a colony or a valuable animal at full precision. Access tiers and stable masking enforce that invariant; random noise does not. Chains should be revisable hypotheses built on immutable observations that each carry two times: when the animal was seen and when the platform recorded it. Links between observations come from additive evidence and are confirmed by people. Photo re-identification can only rank candidates for people to check, because even on curated face crops the best published model's first guess is right only **77.9% of the time for dogs and 54.3% for cats**. The team's FastAPI, Postgres, Next.js and Docker Compose baseline fits with targeted additions, at roughly **US$0–300/month for an MVP and US$3–6k/month near one million monthly users**. The opening is real: no Brazilian platform dominates, and the federal SinPatinhas registry covers about 2% of owned dogs and cats. The chosen name is free on every domain checked and at INPI. Several same-concept apps launched in 2026, so defensive filing is urgent. Nearly every numeric parameter below is borrowed from other countries or domains and must be recalibrated on the platform's own data within its first months.

## Brazil has proven demand but no default platform

### Abroad, aggregators with an institutional anchor won

The strongest international model is the one that stopped competing with existing channels. Petco Love Lost is a free, nonprofit-funded US database with photo matching, and it passed **250,000 reunions in April 2026** ([Pet Age](https://www.petage.com/?p=503153)). Since May 2024 it has automatically ingested every pet-related Nextdoor post ([Nextdoor](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2024/05/30/reunite-lost-pets-beyond-your-neighborhood-with-petco-love)), and city animal services such as Sacramento's use it as their official lost-and-found map ([City of Sacramento](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/community-development/animal-care/lost-and-found)). Even this leader has a feedback-loop problem: a reunion counts only when the person who posted reports it, and Petco Love's COO estimates that **only about one-third of reunions are ever confirmed** ([Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/?p=44893)).

Amazon's Ring shows the opposite risk. Its Search Party feature scans neighbors' outdoor cameras for a reported dog, and Amazon says it reunites "more than one lost dog a day" ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-search-party-for-dogs-united-states-missing-pets)). But the feature is switched on by default ([Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/company/ring/)). Ring's 2026 Super Bowl ad drew a surveillance backlash, after which Ring dropped a planned partnership to share camera footage with law enforcement ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/ring-flock-partnership-amazon-scraps-surveillance-company-safety-super-bowl-commercial-backlash/18596207/)).

At the other extreme, Bogotá's 2025 municipal portal is a bulletin board whose operator explicitly neither verifies reports nor intermediates contact between users ([Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2025/08/19/distrito-lanzo-portal-web-para-mascotas-perdidas-en-bogota-asi-funciona/)). That leaves trust, stale reports, fraud and matching all unsolved. Scams aimed at owners of lost pets run across the whole category. In one common version, a fake finder asks for a six-digit "verification" code that actually hijacks the owner's phone number ([CBS Colorado](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/scammers-targeting-coloradans-castle-rock-missing-pets/)).

The pattern to copy has four parts: aggregate the channels people already use, anchor the platform in an institution, make precise location opt-in, and build in scam resistance from day one. The pattern to avoid is surveillance switched on by default.

### Brazilian attempts launch, stall, and leave Facebook and WhatsApp in charge

The research found **no national-scale lost-pet or stray-animal platform that dominates in Brazil**. Startups have launched and faded quietly. Pupz (2022) asked owners for six face photos and claimed more than 94% recognition accuracy, a figure that comes from the company itself, which also sold GPS and QR collars ([Mobile Time](https://www.mobiletime.com.br/?p=698265)). ILY in Belo Horizonte (2023) sent alerts **through WhatsApp** to registered protetores in nearby postal codes and validated each profile by its WhatsApp number ([Estado de Minas](https://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2023/01/24/interna_gerais,1448600/plataforma-mineira-ajuda-a-encontrar-pets-perdidos.shtml)). The current status of both is unknown.

During the 2024 Rio Grande do Sul floods, about 10,000 animals had been rescued by mid-May. A couple in Canoas built an AI matcher on Monday.com within days ([TecMundo](https://www.tecmundo.com.br/software/282908-rs-casal-lanca-sistema-ia-match-entre-pets-perdidos-tutores-veja.htm); [Olhar Digital](https://olhardigital.com.br/2024/05/16/pro/ia-ajuda-tutores-a-encontrar-animais-desaparecidos-apos-enchentes-no-rs/)). It ran alongside separate registries from the state, São Paulo city hall and NGOs. The demand was proven, and then the tools vanished.

Brazilian giving is driven by emergencies. Half the population donated to an emergency in 2024 ([IDIS](https://www.idis.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/InfograficoDigital_PDB_2024.pdf)), and a single Vakinha flood campaign raised about R$80 million from more than 1.2 million donors ([Startups.com.br](https://startups.com.br/negocios/crowdfunding/pioneiro-do-crowdfunding-vakinha-se-firma-como-plataforma-de-impacto/)). Alerta Patinha therefore needs a crisis mode that exists before the next disaster: simplified intake, triage queues and a welcome flow for newcomers.

In 2026 alone, several same-concept apps appeared on Google Play, each with **10+ installs**. They include AchaPet ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=br.com.doglost.app)), BichoAí, which calls itself "o Waze colaborativo da proteção animal" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=br.com.bichoai.bichoai_app)), and EncontraPet. Meanwhile, real behavior runs elsewhere. There are Facebook groups with "hundreds of new posts per day" ([Catraca Livre](https://catracalivre.com.br/carrefour-causa-animal/como-encontrar-um-animal-perdido-veja-dicas-e-servicos-de-buscas/)), WhatsApp communities run by newsrooms, such as Folha Vitória's "Cadê meu pet?" ([Folha Vitória](https://www.folhavitoria.com.br/geral/cade-meu-pet-voce-viu-o-tutor-cao-foi-encontrado-perdido-na-serra/)), and protetores who amplify cases themselves. These channels reach people. But they have no sense of geography or time, cases are never closed, and the public phone numbers they rely on invite fraud.

Why none of these became the default is inference, but the pieces fit. Lost-pet tools have local network effects: they help only if enough people nearby are already watching, and Facebook and WhatsApp already have that audience. Install friction is steep. A deployment of the civic app Colab in Saquarema logged 6,902 downloads against only 1,392 requests in a year ([Diário Oficial de Saquarema](https://dos.saquarema.rj.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/D.O.S._1642-7_assinado.pdf)). No Brazilian attempt found a revenue or endowment model comparable to Petco Love's. None integrated with a CCZ or with SinPatinhas, so none became the "official" channel. The design consequence is that Alerta Patinha's unit of value is a canonical case link that people share *into* the groups they already use. WhatsApp intake creates the structured record behind that link.

### SinPatinhas covers about 2% of owned animals and offers no public API

SinPatinhas, launched by the Ministry of the Environment in April 2025, is a free, voluntary registry that requires a Gov.br login ([CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/politica/sinpatinhas-como-cadastrar-caes-e-gatos-no-programa-de-rg-para-pets/)). It issues an "RG Animal" with a QR code that a finder can scan to reach the owner's authorized contact ([Congresso em Foco](https://congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/107835/sinpatinhas-entenda-o-novo-rg-animal-lancado-pelo-governo-federal)). At one year it held **1,305,529 animals** and was present in **98.3% of municipalities** ([Tribuna de Minas](https://tribunademinas.com.br/colunas/bichoseacidade/03-05-2026/sinpatinhas-reune-informacoes-da-populacao-de-caes-e-gatos-no-brasil.html?amp=1)). Press reports of federal data put it at about **1.8 million by September 2026** ([Portal de Prefeitura](https://portaldeprefeitura.com.br/pernambuco/pernambuco-animais-cadastrados-sinpatinhas-castracao-microchipagem/634008/)). Against **62.2 million dogs and 30.8 million cats** in Brazil ([CartaCapital](https://www.cartacapital.com.br/?p=442972)), that is about 2% coverage.

SinPatinhas registers owned animals. Strays and community animals mostly fall outside it, unless a municipality registers them to receive federal castration funds, as Campinas now requires ([Correio da Manhã](https://www.correiodamanha.com.br/nacional/sao-paulo/campinas/2026/04/amp/281693-mutirao-de-castracao-em-campinas-utiliza-sistema-federal-sinpatinhas-para-cadastro-de-pets.html)). The research found no public API or open-data release. Alerta Patinha should treat SinPatinhas as an integration to pursue with the ministry, never as a dependency. Examples are scanning the RG Animal QR code on a found animal and nudging reunited owners to register. The platform should never mirror the registry's "negativação" list of people responsible for mistreatment.

Brazil's animal statistics need the same care. The widely repeated figure of "30 million abandoned animals (WHO)" has no traceable WHO source ([Queiroz & Silva 2024](https://repositorio.ifap.edu.br/jspui/bitstream/prefix/920/1/QUEIROZ%2c%20SILVA%20%282024%29%20-%20Diagn%c3%b3stico%20em%20prol.pdf)) and should not appear in Alerta Patinha's materials. The best available figure is Instituto Pet Brasil's estimate of **about 4.8 million dogs and cats "in vulnerability" in 2024**, plus about 201,000 in the care of NGOs or protetores ([CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/quase-5-milhoes-de-pets-estao-em-situacao-de-vulnerabilidade-no-brasil/)). "In vulnerability" means animals of families below the poverty line, or animals on the street that receive some community care. The series swings from 3.9M to 8.8M to 4.8M across editions, and its dog/cat split flipped, so it is reliable only as an order of magnitude. Nobody measures street-animal populations directly. Alerta Patinha's chains, once deduplicated and geotagged, could therefore become Brazil's first continuous record of where strays are. That is a public good worth designing for.

### Users are phone-only, prepaid, data-capped and on WhatsApp

TIC Domicílios 2025 finds that **99% of internet users connect by phone and 65% connect only by phone** (87% in classes D and E). It also finds that **52% of phone owners are on prepaid plans**, and that **39% ran out of mobile data at least once in three months** ([Cetic.br](https://www.cetic.br/media/docs/publicacoes/2/pt-br/20260511173151/tic_domicilios_2025_resumoexecutivo%20pt.pdf)). Instant messaging reaches 92% of internet users, and 84% even in classes D and E ([Cetic.br C5](https://cetic.br/en/tics/domicilios/2025/individuos/C5/)). Among WhatsApp users, 97% open it daily ([Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/o-que-os-brasileiros-mais-fazem-no-whatsapp-segundo-pesquisa/)). Android holds about 78% of the mobile market ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/brazil)). Many prepaid plans still don't count WhatsApp against the data allowance, but that is a revocable promotion, and carrier executives openly discuss ending it ([Estado de Minas](https://www.em.com.br/emfoco/?p=17985)). Page weight and photo size are therefore product decisions. Photos should be compressed on the device, reports should queue when offline, and WhatsApp should be the default channel for intake and alerts. Case links need rich previews (photo, status, bairro), because WhatsApp is where those links will travel.

### Brazilian law decides what may be published and who may act

Two federal statutes set the field. Lei 14.228/2021 bars zoonoses agencies from killing dogs and cats except for serious, incurable infectious disease backed by a technical report, and it gives animal-protection entities access to that justification ([Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2019-2022/2021/lei/l14228.htm)). Lei 14.064/2020 ("Lei Sansão") raised the penalty for mistreating dogs and cats to **2–5 years of reclusão** ([Dizer o Direito](https://www.dizerodireito.com.br/2020/09/lei-140642020-aumenta-pena-do-crime-de.html?m=1)).

Community-animal laws give a named caretaker a formal role. São Paulo's Lei 12.916/2008 returns a sterilized community dog to its territory once its *cuidador principal* signs a commitment term ([ALESP](https://www.al.sp.gov.br/repositorio/legislacao/lei/2008/lei-12916-16.04.2008.html)). Rio Grande do Sul's Lei 13.193/2009 created the "cão comunitário," and Porto Alegre opened a municipal community-animal registry in February 2026 ([Prefeitura de Porto Alegre](https://prefeitura.poa.br/smgg/noticias/prefeitura-fortalece-politica-de-protecao-caes-comunitarios-por-meio-de-cadastro)). Community animals are therefore a distinct entity type, with a territory and caretakers. A sighting of one must not trigger a "lost" or "rescue" flow, or well-meaning users will remove animals from caretakers the law recognizes.

Animal photos are not personal data. Under the LGPD's "identified or identifiable natural person" test, however, exact coordinates near a home, reporter identities, chat content, and faces or license plates in photos all are ([LGPD](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)). Legitimate interest, with a documented balancing test, is the natural legal basis for the civic map. Consent is the natural basis for optional sharing of precise location. Marco Civil art. 15 requires for-profit application providers to keep access logs for six months ([Marco Civil](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2014/lei/l12965.htm)). A nonprofit operator could argue it falls outside that rule, but six months of retention is the safe default.

The STF's June 2025 ruling on art. 19 made platforms liable for third-party illicit content they fail to remove after being notified, and requires immediate action for the gravest categories ([Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/26/06/2025/art-19-stf-concilia-votos-e-plataformas-terao-responsabilizacao-parcial/)). It kept the court-order requirement for crimes against honor ([BMA](https://www.bmalaw.com.br/conteudo/institucional/informativos-e-newsletters/constitucionalidade-do-artigo-19-do-marco-civil-da-internet/pdf)). Appeals asking the court to clarify the ruling were still pending in mid-2026 ([STF Notícias](https://noticias.stf.jus.br/postsnoticias/stf-vai-analisar-recursos-contra-tese-fixada-no-julgamento-do-marco-civil-da-internet/)). That carve-out protects the operator, not its users. TJMG upheld R$4,000 in moral damages plus a public retraction against a user who used social media to accuse someone of a crime and publish their personal data, in a dispute over a rescued dog ([TJMG](https://bd.tjmg.jus.br/bitstreams/f05e811d-7c53-4a30-95ca-8fd451a1b93d/download)). TJSC has awarded damages for social-media attacks on a protetora ([TJSC](https://www.tjsc.jus.br/web/imprensa/noticias/-/asset_publisher/GP1QtxFaSsX0/content/id/15681776)).

Finally, the ECA Digital has applied since 17 March 2026 to services "likely to be accessed" by minors. It requires age verification and protective defaults ([Jornal de Brasília](https://jornaldebrasilia.com.br/noticias/brasil/eca-digital-entra-em-vigor-nesta-terca-17-veja-o-que-muda-para-plataformas-e-usuarios/)). A free map with chat between strangers about animals plausibly qualifies, since teenagers often feed strays.

Together these rules call for a notice-and-takedown workflow with response-time targets and a record of each decision, no public naming of alleged abusers, a private channel that routes mistreatment reports to the authorities, defaults that are safe for minors, and six months of access-log retention.

The details of the STF ruling and the ECA Digital implementing decrees still need a lawyer's reading.

## Participation follows a power law, so build two products

### A small core does most of the work, and the second report is the conversion that matters

Citizen science gives the realistic baseline. Across seven Zooniverse projects, 100,386 people contributed 129,500 hours, and most of them took part only once ([Sauermann & Franzoni 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4311847)). The top 10% did **almost 80% of the work**, and on average only **27% of users came back** for a second session ([Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/?p=44784)). Of the newcomers to iNaturalist's City Nature Challenge in 2019, only 15–20% were still active in 2020 ([arXiv 2112.02693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02693)).

Alerta Patinha will have the same shape. Most people will file exactly one emergency or sighting, and in each metro area a core of protetores and NGO staff will build most chains. That calls for two products with different success criteria. One is a one-shot reporter flow, whose job is to cost almost nothing. The other is a professional case tool for the core, whose job is to save time and protect their capacity. The most useful headline metric is the **share of first-time reporters who file a second report**. A rate of 25–30% would match citizen-science norms.

The core user is not who a growth team would assume. By IBGE's strict measure, 4.2% of people aged 14 and over did volunteer work in 2022. The rate was higher among women (4.9%), people over 50 (4.8%) and university graduates (6.9%) ([IBGE](https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv102020_informativo.pdf)). IDIS/Datafolha uses a looser definition and counts 34% as active volunteers ([CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/em-dez-anos-perfil-do-voluntariado-muda-no-pais-indica-pesquisa/)). The animal cause grew from 1% to 9% of the causes volunteers chose between 2011 and 2021 ([GIFE](https://sinapse.gife.org.br/dlm_file/guia-de-boas-praticas-da-causa-animal)). Ethnography of Rio's protetores shows people recruited through Facebook and their social circles. It also shows a "gift" ethic that resists turning the work into a profession ([Osório 2016](https://www.scielo.br/j/vb/a/pQ5bpKbhCWCT6bv8cGkzchC/?lang=en); [Osório 2017](https://periodicos.ufs.br/Ambivalencias/article/download/7508/6767/24184)).

Together these suggest the core contributor is most likely an older woman, using an Android phone, living inside WhatsApp, and motivated by care rather than status. Large tap targets, readable type and WhatsApp-first flows serve her; verification that feels bureaucratic drives her away. New capacity should come from a growing layer of casual helpers who confirm sightings, transport animals or feed them, because the existing protetores are already overloaded.

### Friction, defaults and the honest size of nudges

The best predictor of whether people start and finish a survey is how long it *says* it will take ([Statistics Canada, citing Galesic & Bosnjak](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/2013002/article/11885/section4-eng.htm)). The emergency flow should therefore be photo, automatic location, send, with an explicit promise ("leva 10 segundos"). Everything else, such as species, condition and contact, should be optional, inferred from the photo, or asked after submission. Account creation belongs after the first report, framed by the reporter's own motivation: "quer saber o que aconteceu com esse animal?"

Defaults are among the sturdier levers. They averaged an effect of d ≈ 0.63–0.68 across 58 studies, but individual effects ranged from −0.5 to 2 ([Jachimowicz et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/bpubpo/v3y2019i02p159-186_00.html)). Sensible defaults here are an approximate public location, updates switched on for your own reports, and alerts about nearby cases switched off until the user opts in.

The broader nudge literature is inflated. Trials run by US government "nudge units" produce effects **about one-sixth** the size of those in published academic papers ([Verfassungsblog on DellaVigna & Linos](https://verfassungsblog.de/nudging-after-the-replication-crisis/)). The foot-in-the-door effect, where a small first request makes people more likely to agree to a bigger one, is weak ([Beaman et al. 1983](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167283092002)). A "ladder of asks" (confirm, photograph, bring water, transport, foster) is still worth building, but because each step takes less effort, not because it boosts compliance. Every nudge is a hypothesis, so infrastructure for A/B testing should exist from launch.

Duplicates are the other friction point. In a trial, FixMyStreet offered users a subscription to an existing nearby report instead of filing a new one ([mySociety](https://www.mysociety.org/2019/04/24/stop-right-there-thats-already-been-reported/)). The equivalent here is "É este animal? Adicione sua foto ao caso," which turns what would have been a rejection into a contribution to the chain.

Notifications need the same restraint. Phones already receive a median of 56 notifications a day ([Pielot et al. 2018](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Pielot-MobileHCI18.pdf)). In an RCT with 237 people, batching notifications three times a day improved attention, mood and sense of control. Turning notifications off entirely raised anxiety and fear of missing out ([Fitz et al. 2019](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/batching-notifications-can-improve-attention-well-being-and-productivity)). Real-time push should be reserved for nearby emergencies and updates on the user's own cases. Everything else goes into a digest sent at most one to three times a day, with a user-set radius, quiet hours and a per-chain mute.

### Closing the loop with photos is the best-evidenced lever

Across 399,364 FixMyStreet users, a successful first experience was associated with a **57% higher probability of a second report**. How responsive the service was to the first report predicted all later reporting ([Sjoberg, Mellon & Peixoto 2017](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2bd169fd-0b27-46e4-8b81-1ccfccf7c55a)). In Boston, residents who received photos of the city resolving their requests submitted **60% more requests across 38% more categories** over 13 months. Showing a growing backlog of unmet requests left trust no lower than showing nothing at all ([Buell, Porter & Norton 2021](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/msom.2020.0877)).

The FixMyStreet result is observational, but the two studies converge on a design. Push the chain's latest outcome photo to everyone who contributed: the animal at the vet, in a lar temporário, or back with its tutor. Credit contributors by name ("sua foto de 14/10 ajudou a localizar o Thor"). Show an honest status on unresolved cases ("6 h sem ajuda, 3 pessoas notificadas"). And move first-time reporters' cases to the front of responder queues, because the first experience carries disproportionate weight.

Brazil adds a twist. Only about **20% of Brazilians trust local government**, and most doubt that institutions act on feedback ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-in-brazil_fb0e1896-en/full-report/component-5.html)). The platform itself must reliably close the loop, and it must not promise municipal action it cannot guarantee. Asking people to confirm closure also produces measurement: Petco's experience shows that reunions nobody is prompted to report are undercounted roughly threefold.

### Bystanders become helpers through proximity, a specific ask and a visible claimant

Some popular persuasion effects are weaker than folklore suggests. The identifiable-victim effect, the idea that one named victim draws more help than many, averages r = .05 across 41 studies, and the best-powered studies find close to nothing ([Lee & Feeley 2016](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/the-identifiable-victim-effect-a-meta-analytic-review/)). Compassion does fade from the second victim onward ([Västfjäll et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4062481/)). Showing one animal per ask is therefore cheap insurance, and "37 animais precisam de ajuda perto de você" is the wrong call to action. The bystander effect is real in laboratory meta-analysis (g = −0.35) ([Fischer et al. 2011](https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/20599/)). Yet CCTV footage of 219 real public conflicts shows that someone intervened in about 9 of 10 ([Philpot et al. 2020](https://gwern.net/doc/crime/2020-philpot.pdf)).

The best-evidenced countermeasure comes from cardiac-arrest dispatch. Alerting trained volunteers within 500 m raised bystander CPR from **48% to 62%** in a Stockholm RCT ([Ringh et al. 2015](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2015/06/10/16/09/Mobile-Phone-Dispatch-of-Laypersons-for-CPR-in-OHCA)). The funnel is steep. Of 1,985 alerted responders in Australia and New Zealand, 75.6% saw the alert, 37.7% accepted, 27.1% arrived and **14.3% provided care** ([Monash](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-binational-survey-of-smartphone-activated-volunteer-responders-/)). That is about seven alerts per person who acts. Animal emergencies feel lower-stakes and often need transport or money, so they will likely do worse.

For Alerta Patinha, the translation is a specific, personal ask sent to a handful of nearby helpers who have opted in ("Você está a ~400 m. Pode confirmar se o cão ainda está lá?"). An "Estou indo" button shows who has claimed the case and stops the alerts to everyone else. And every report confirmation offers a next action, so that reporting never feels like the end of the job.

### Recognition should be symbolic, and points, leaderboards and streaks backfire

Symbolic recognition works. Randomly assigned, purely symbolic awards on Wikipedia raised newcomer retention for a year ([Gallus 2017](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2540)). Giving top editors a peer award (a "barnstar") raised their productivity by about 60% over 90 days ([Restivo & van de Rijt 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315525)).

Engineered incentives are weaker or harmful. Only about 20% of Stack Overflow users respond to badges ([Hoernle et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.06160)). In a meta-analysis of programming education, badges, points and leaderboards had no significant effect on learning ([ISCTE](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/29213/1/article_95533.pdf)). Incentives tied directly to performance shift effort toward quantity over quality ([Cerasoli et al. 2014](https://oa.mg/work/10.1037/a0035661)). And highlighting a broken streak reduces later engagement ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)).

On Alerta Patinha, points per report would pay people for duplicate sightings and bad photos, and those would contaminate the training data for photo matching. Streaks would break for nearly everyone, because emergencies are not a daily habit. Recognition should come from people: a thank-you from the protetor who resolved the case, acknowledgments on the chain, and a history of the animals each person has helped.

How newcomers are treated matters just as much. Wikipedia's decline followed a rise in good-faith newcomers having their edits reverted, a pattern replicated across 769 wikis ([Halfaker et al. 2013](https://stuartgeiger.com/papers/abs-rise-and-decline-wikipedia.pdf)). On Alerta Patinha, the equivalent is a veteran answering "isso já foi resolvido" or "foto inútil" in a chain chat. Veterans need ready-made templates for correcting newcomers gently, and flawed reports from newcomers should go to a review queue rather than being deleted.

### Emotion, outrage and burnout need structural answers

Content warnings do not reduce distress. A meta-analysis found that they raise anxiety in advance and have a trivial effect on the emotional response itself (d = 0.02) ([Bridgland et al. 2023](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/2023-october-content-warnings-distress.html)). Most people uncover Instagram-style blur screens anyway ([PsyPost on Flinders](https://www.psypost.org/instagrams-content-warning-screens-may-increase-anxiety-study-finds/)). Blur is therefore a tool for consent and context, not a wellbeing feature. The public card should lead with a photo that identifies the animal (face or body) and a factual status line. Close-ups of wounds should be an attachment for responders that opens on tap.

Outrage is a structural risk. Each moral-emotional word in a tweet raised how far it spread by about 20% ([Brady et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514704)). Press coverage of Pesquisa Doação Brasil 2024 reports that 49% of Brazilian donors have stopped giving after negative news; this figure should be checked against the full survey ([Monitor Mercantil](https://monitormercantil.com.br/brasileira-estara-na-lista-time100-philanthropy-2026/)). Structured update blocks (status, location, needs) should be the main way a case is updated, with free chat secondary. Shareable posts should carry an action ("precisa de transporte até 18h"), not blame.

Burnout is the last structural risk. Among shelter volunteers, the biggest stressor was an open-intake policy, meaning every animal that arrived had to be taken in ([Faunalytics](https://faunalytics.org/compassion-fatigue-in-animal-shelter-volunteers/)). That is exactly what Alerta Patinha becomes if it routes every emergency to the same few known protetores. Showing each helper's caseload, letting them flag capacity, supporting handoffs, offering a guilt-free "pausa" mode, and routing cases to spread the load are therefore welfare mechanisms. Their health shows up in one metric: how evenly caseload is spread across helpers, measured with a Gini coefficient.

## Mechanism design must assume free riders, scammers and turf wars

### Sightings are a public good, and public reputation scores rot

A sighting is a public good. One person's use doesn't reduce anyone else's and nobody can be excluded, yet it costs the reporter time, mobile data and emotional load. Free riding is therefore the default, and a small group that cares intensely carries the load. The design answer has two parts: push the casual reporter's cost toward zero, and give the core group tools rather than points.

Reputation systems for that core decay in predictable ways. On one online labor market, perfect ratings rose from 33% to 85% in six years. Between 35% and 45% of that rise came from raters becoming laxer, and the inflation began when feedback became public ([Filippas & Horton](https://cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp6750.pdf)). On eBay, the median seller has 100% positive feedback ([Filippas et al., NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25857.pdf)). In a small, territorial field where everyone knows each other, people will rarely post public negative feedback about an ONG or protetor, and public star ratings would escalate turf conflicts.

Reputation should therefore be based on outcomes, coarse, and private. Score each report against what eventually happened (found, rescued, reunited, or confirmed absent). Use the score to decide who is alerted first and how much a confirmation vote weighs. Never count "animals received," which would reward the accumulation that slides into hoarding.

Elegant incentive mechanisms from economics fail here. Peer prediction and the Bayesian Truth Serum pay people for reports that match what others report. They need large populations who share the same prior beliefs, and usually money ([Faltings, Jurca & Radanovic](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.05269)). In an online experiment, participants coordinated on reports that carried no information ([Gao et al. 2014](https://econcs.seas.harvard.edu/resource/gaoec14pdf)). A handful of people who saw one dog on one corner meet none of those conditions.

Cheaper tools work. Waze shortens a report's life when users tap "not there," and unconfirmed reports expire ([Waze Help](https://support.google.com/waze/answer/13739290?hl=en)). Here, that becomes a sighting whose confidence decays over time and responds to "ainda está lá / não está mais" votes. Only people physically nearby can vote, and each vote is weighted by the voter's trust. iNaturalist grants "Research Grade" only when more than two-thirds of at least two identifiers agree, and the status can be revoked later ([iNaturalist](https://help.inaturalist.org/support/solutions/articles/151000169936)). That is a good rule for "same animal?" decisions, but too slow for emergencies. Brazil's Fogo Cruzado accepts anonymous gunfire reports and checks them against other reports and police data before publishing ([Exame](https://exame.com/brasil/apps-sao-usados-para-trocar-informacoes-sobre-tiroteios-no-rio)). It is the right template for anything that names places tied to people.

Stopping one person from running many fake accounts (a Sybil attack) requires a central identity authority ([Douceur](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-sybil-attack/)). Phone verification is therefore the minimum. Higher tiers need a CNPJ or a Gov.br login, and vouching by other users is never the only gate. Evidence quality should also be ranked: an in-app "foto ao vivo," taken with the device's location and the server's clock, outranks a photo picked from the gallery. Photo metadata is trivially editable, and AI-edited images already appear in Brazilian scams.

### Coordinate with expanding rings, expiring claims and a stop rule

Dispatch is a coordination game with two bad outcomes: everyone waits for someone else, or several people converge on the same animal. Volunteer-dispatch data from cardiac arrests show both. In London's 2023/24 GoodSAM data, volunteers **accepted 22.4% of alerts, and 47.5% of those who accepted never arrived** ([London Ambulance Service](https://www.londonambulance.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Cardiac-Arrest-Annual-Report-2023-24-1-1.pdf)). An earlier study found acceptance as low as 1.3% of London cases ([PMC8757292](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8757292/)). Victoria, Australia alerts the three nearest volunteers within 400–500 m in metro areas and **keeps alerting until three accept** ([MJA 2025](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2025/222/10/smartphone-activated-volunteer-responders-and-survival-discharge-after-out)). Denmark's HeartRunner tells a volunteer who moves out of range that they are no longer needed ([HeartRunner protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/03/NCT03835403/Prot_SAP_002.pdf)). Better final outcomes don't always follow. One RCT found no increase in bystander use of defibrillators ([2 Minute Medicine on SAMBA](https://www.2minutemedicine.com/visualabstract-smartphone-dispatch-of-volunteer-responders-may-not-increase-bystander-use-of-automated-external-defibrillator-in-out-of-hospital-cardiac-arrest/)). Copenhagen saw more bystander defibrillation but no difference in 30-day survival ([TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/smartphone-app-boosts-bystander-response-cardiac-arrest)). Success should therefore be measured as resolution (the animal reached, transported or reunited), not as alerts sent.

The mechanism that follows has four parts. First, alerts go out in expanding rings with a stop rule. The nearest qualified users are alerted in a small ring (about 300–500 m in cities), and the ring widens step by step toward 2–5 km depending on urgency. **Alerting stops once N people accept**, where N is 1–3 depending on severity, and each user has a daily alert budget and quiet hours. That handles both failure modes: in dense centers several protetores converging causes friction and fatigue, while in periferias nobody may answer.

Second, claims expire. "Estou a caminho" carries an ETA and is released automatically at the ETA plus a buffer unless the claimer checks in. At most two people can hold a claim at once, because a single claim followed by a no-show is the worst case. Releasing a claim before it expires ("não vou conseguir") carries no penalty.

Third, an unclaimed case says so explicitly and personally ("você é a pessoa mais próxima; 0 pessoas a caminho"). After a timeout it escalates to a verified ONG on duty or to the CCZ.

Fourth, responders get safety guidance before dispatch (bite risk, traffic, unsafe areas), are never sent toward conflict between people, and get a check-in after the case. Volunteers dispatched by app to cardiac arrests show measurable psychological impact ([Kragh et al. 2021](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/448968710/Kragh_et_al._2021_._Immediate_psychological_impact_on_citizen_responders_dispatched_through_a_mobile_application_to_out_of_hospital_cardiac_arrests.pdf)).

Equity is the hidden variable. App-based help concentrates where smartphones and free time do, while hoarding and abandonment concentrate elsewhere. That is why WhatsApp intake and escalation to institutions matter, and why coverage should be measured by region and income from the pilot onward. The radius, N and the timeouts are hypotheses: no study tests claims with timeouts in animal rescue.

### Every documented abuse vector combines location, contact and identity

Brazilian news reports document each threat the platform must design against.

The "golpe do pet perdido" targets owners who post a photo and phone number publicly. A fake finder demands R$100–150 by Pix for fuel or food ([Folha do Interior](https://www.folhadointerior.com.br/golpistas-usam-animais-perdidos-para-enganar-tutores-e-pedir-dinheiro-via-pix/)). In 2026, scammers began using AI to edit the owner's own pet photo into "found" scenes, and threatened owners who asked for a live video ([Estado de Minas](http://www.em.com.br/gerais/2026/07/7458985-golpe-com-inteligencia-artificial-atinge-tutores-de-animais-perdidos-em-bh.html)). One case in Ponta Grossa escalated to threats to kill the dog ([Metrópoles](https://www.metropoles.com/colunas/mirelle-pinheiro/golpista-usa-ia-para-fingir-resgate-de-cachorro-e-engana-familia)). Victims rarely recover their money: only **8.9% of Pix scam losses** between January 2022 and April 2026 were returned ([O POVO+](https://mais.opovo.com.br/reportagens-especiais/2026/07/24/a-cada-rs-100-em-golpes-no-pix-instituicoes-devolvem-apenas-rs-890.html)).

Donation fraud is organized. A couple allegedly created **234 Pix keys** resembling protetores' keys to divert flood donations in 2024 ([Baguete](https://www.baguete.com.br/noticias/casal-de-atores-e-preso-por-golpe-com-pix)).

Animals are deliberately targeted. Cat colonies are poisoned with chumbinho: ten cats died at a Belo Horizonte ONG, and thirty animals in Cariacica ([SBT News](https://sbtnews.sbt.com.br/noticia/policia/policia-investiga-a-morte-de-10-gatos-por-envenenamento-em-ong-de-bh); [CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/sudeste/es/homem-e-preso-por-vender-chumbinho-apos-30-animais-morrerem-envenenados/)). Purebred dogs are stolen at gunpoint ([SBT News](https://sbtnews.sbt.com.br/noticia/brasil/policia-procura-caes-de-raca-levados-em-assalto-em-guarulhos)), and a 2019 raid on a pit-bull fighting ring led to 41 arrests ([Governo do Paraná](https://www.parana.pr.gov.br/Audio/Policia-Civil-do-Parana-prende-40-pessoas-em-rinha-internacional-de-caes)).

Animal hoarding is measurable. Curitiba confirmed 113 hoarding cases (6.45 per 100,000 people), 36.3% of them involving animals, clustered in denser, lower-income areas ([Cunha et al. 2017](https://scielosp.org/article/csp/2017.v33n2/e00001316)).

No case was found linking any platform's sighting data to a theft or poisoning, so that link remains a plausible risk rather than a documented one. Conservation shows how fast it can materialize: published locations of newly described species enabled poaching within months ([ANU](https://science.anu.edu.au/news-events/news/scientists-urged-keep-rare-species-habitat-secret)).

The cautionary tale on crowd dynamics is Citizen, an app that launched under the name "Vigilante." In May 2021 it livestreamed a manhunt for a supposed arson suspect, raised a $30,000 reward, and pushed it to 848,816 Los Angeles users. The man was innocent. Leaked messages tied the hunt to signup growth, and staff were measured on how fast and how many incidents they published ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/inside-crime-app-citizen-vigilante)). The LAPD cut ties afterward ([Techdirt](https://archive.techdirt.com/articles/20220129/19233148382/emails-show-lapd-cut-ties-with-citizen-app-after-started-vigilante-manhunt-targeting-innocent-person.shtml)).

Alerta Patinha must never point the crowd at people. That means no bounties, no "find the abuser" threads, no faces or addresses of suspects, and no staff targets based on how fast or how much gets published. Nextdoor's structured, multi-step form for crime posts claimed a 75% drop in racial profiling. That is the company's own figure, without independent verification ([Nextdoor](https://blog.nextdoor.com/2016/08/24/reducing-racial-profiling-on-nextdoor)). A similar deliberately slower form is the right home for mistreatment reports. They should be routed privately to the delegacia, the Ministério Público or the municipal channel, with an export the reporter can use to file a boletim de ocorrência.

| Exposed element | Who exploits it | Harm | Minimum control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner's phone and name on a lost-pet case | Scammer, extortionist | Pix losses, threats | Messages relayed through the platform; no public phone; inline warning on money requests |
| High-resolution photos showing unique marks | Scammer with AI editing | Convincing fake "found" proof | Lower-resolution, watermarked public photos; 1–2 "secret marks" kept private for ownership challenges |
| Exact home location of a tutor or feeder | Stalker, thief, harasser | Doxxing, burglary | Never published; snapped to a coarse cell; user-declared private zones |
| Exact colony or feeding point | Poisoner, hostile neighbor or condomínio | Mass killing | Hidden automatically; exact location only for the caretaker and verified organizations |
| Location of valuable breeds or pit-bull-type dogs | Thieves, fighting rings | Theft | Location coarsened by breed; delayed publication; precision only for verified users |
| Identity of an alleged abuser | Mob, vigilante | Harassment, defamation liability | Structured private report to authorities; no names, faces or addresses in public |
| Pix key in chat or on a campaign | Fraudster with a look-alike key | Diverted donations | Verified recipients only; copy button showing the holder's name; no typed-in keys |

Money is what makes each of these harms worse, so at launch no money flows through the platform. Users reach each other only through an in-app or relayed channel. A "found" claim on a lost pet triggers a proof-of-possession challenge: a live video with a gesture the owner requests, or answers about private marks. Chat is scanned for Pix keys, phone numbers and phrases such as "manda pix," "taxa," "frete" and "gasolina." A match shows a warning rather than blocking the message, because people legitimately discuss vet costs. The first contact on any case shows a banner saying that nobody from the platform will ask for codes and that nobody should pay before seeing the animal. Owners can display "recompensa oferecida (paga só na entrega)" as text, but the platform never holds money. When donations arrive later, they go only to verified recipients (a CNPJ, or a verified CPF with lower limits). Vet invoices are posted as updates on the chain, which addresses the loss of trust that makes donors quit after bad news.

### Moderate by harm class on a progressive trust ladder

Experiments in online communities give clear rules. Posting the community's rules in 2,190 r/science discussions raised newcomers' rule compliance by more than 8 percentage points and their participation by about 70% ([Matias 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6525516)). Explaining why posts were removed lowered future removals, and explanations sent by a bot worked about as well as human ones ([Discourse Meta summary of Jhaver et al.](https://meta.discourse.org/t/transparency-in-moderation-really-matters/133261)).

Moderation should be split by how much harm a wrong decision causes, not by volume. Low-harm, reversible decisions go to trusted community members: duplicates, merging and splitting chains, staleness, and consensus on species and identity. High-harm decisions go to staff, with response-time targets: doxxing, accusations against identifiable people, Pix fraud and extortion, overriding a colony's location protection, impersonation of ONGs or CCZs, and welfare concerns about hoarding. Staff take these for two reasons. Local volunteers in a territorial field have conflicts of interest. And since the STF ruling, liability for content left up after notice falls on the operator.

Privileges should unlock with verified outcomes, not with time on the platform or post volume, across roughly five rungs. A visitor sees the coarse map. A phone-verified user can post sightings and emergencies, subject to rate limits, and can chat. A reliable reporter casts weighted confirmations and proposes links between sightings. A trusted local protetor or mantenedor, vouched for by two trusted users and reviewed by staff, sees exact locations in their own territory, merges chains and marks animals as "comunitário." A verified organization (holding a CNPJ, or a government email domain in the case of a CCZ) carries an official badge, receives donations through verified Pix keys and gets escalated cases.

Sanctions escalate from an explanation, to temporary limits, to demotion, to a ban, and each step is explained. Some accounts will keep taking in animals with no adoption, transfer or vet visit to show for it. A confidential welfare check, reviewed by a person, addresses that hoarding risk without public stigma. Verified accounts for CCZs and municipalities provide the outside recognition that Ostrom's design principles, drawn from long-lived shared resources, say such communities need ([Cox et al.](https://www.lincolninst.edu/app/uploads/2024/04/1707_925_Cox20Final.pdf)). Even so, the platform must stay valuable when a change of government cools a partnership.

## Chains are revisable hypotheses over immutable observations

### Movement data set the radii: lost cats hide within 50 metres, lost dogs resolve in two days

The strongest movement evidence is for lost cats. In a survey of 1,210 lost cats, those found alive were a **median of 50 m from where they escaped** (interquartile range 9–500 m). Indoor-only cats were found a median of 39 m away, and cats with outdoor access a median of 300 m away. The cumulative probability of being found alive was 34% by day 7, 50% by day 30 and 56% by day 61, with little gain after that. The authors recommend a search radius of 200 m for indoor-only cats and 2 km or more for cats with outdoor access ([Huang et al. 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5789300/fullTextXML)).

Lost dogs resolve faster. 71% were recovered, after a **median of 2 days**, most often through an animal agency (34.8%), a license tag (18.2%) or neighborhood signs (15.2%) ([Lord et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17223753/)). No peer-reviewed data on how far lost dogs travel was found, so pushing a case to shelters and the CCZ matters more than widening the search circle.

Free-roaming dogs, the closest analog to Brazil's community dogs, have small ranges. GPS tracking of 773 dogs in four countries gave median home ranges of 5.6–7.7 ha, measured as the area containing 95% of each dog's locations ([Warembourg et al. 2021](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC7970034/fullTextXML)). That equals a circle of roughly 135–157 m radius, with outliers reaching about 1 km. In Arequipa, dogs used dry water channels as highways ([Raynor et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/684381v1.full)). Cat colonies are anchored on feeding sites but not closed: in Rome, about 21% of colony members were newcomers, arriving through abandonment or immigration ([Faunalytics on Natoli et al.](https://faunalytics.org/management-of-feral-domestic-cats-in-the-urban-environment-of-rome-italy/)).

No Brazilian urban GPS study of stray dogs or colony cats exists. Brazilian urban form (walled houses, condomínios, dense favelas, open channels) may stretch these numbers. The starting table below is therefore extrapolated from the studies above and must be re-fitted after three to six months of platform data. In the table, the soft and hard link gates are the distance and time within which a new sighting is considered as a possible match for an existing chain (soft) or ruled out entirely beyond (hard). The H3 resolutions are grid cell sizes, explained in the data-model section below.

| Status | Link soft gate | Link hard gate | Location half-life | Case goes dormant | Public display cell | Initial help radius |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency (injured) | 100 m / 6 h | 500 m / 24 h | 2–6 h | Archived after 72 h without an update | Exact in public places while active; H3 res 8 near a home | Ring from ~500 m out to 2–5 km by urgency |
| Lost dog | 1 km, growing | 5 km at 48 h, then citywide plus CCZ | 24–48 h | 60 days | H3 res 9 (exact for chain members) | 3 km, then citywide, shelters and CCZ |
| Lost cat, indoor-only | 200 m | 500 m | 7 days | 90 days | H3 res 9 | 500 m |
| Lost cat, outdoor access | 500 m | 2 km | 7 days | 90 days | H3 res 9 | 2 km |

### Link sightings with additive evidence, and never auto-merge

Wildlife photo identification has already solved the data model. Wildbook treats every upload as an *encounter*: one animal, at one time, in one place. It groups encounters of animals seen together into a *sighting*, and it assigns encounters to a named *individual* only when a person confirms the match. It "assists in photo ID but never makes a decision for the user," and records how each match was made ([Wildbook](https://wildbook.docs.wildme.org/data/matching-process.html)). Alerta Patinha should mirror this. An observation is an immutable fact. A chain is a hypothesis, expressed only through link records that are appended and never edited. A photo of five colony cats creates five observations under one sighting group; otherwise lookalikes will be merged.

Candidate links are scored with Fellegi–Sunter record linkage. Each field contributes log2(m/u) bits of evidence, where m is how often the field agrees for true matches and u is how often it agrees by chance, so agreement on a rare value weighs more ([Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/Lookup/93275A3BB3B61601CA257BE900110E67)). With illustrative parameters, a shared "caramelo" coat adds only about 1.6 bits because the color is so common. A rare merle pattern adds about 5.5 bits, and a confident disagreement on sex subtracts about 3.3 bits. The spatial term comes from the movement data above. For a resident dog, about 95% of pairs of sightings of the same animal fall within about 235 m of each other.

The scores convert to a probability that two sightings show the same animal, and the probability decides what happens. Below 0.2, nothing happens. Between 0.2 and 0.8, the reporter and chain members see a side-by-side card asking "Pode ser o mesmo animal?". Above 0.8, a strong suggestion is pinned for an owner or moderator to confirm. Only trivial cases attach automatically, such as the same reporter in the same session sending a burst of photos. Every yes/no answer is a labeled pair, so the m and u values can be re-estimated per city and species with the EM algorithm, as the Splink library does ([Splink demo](https://nbviewer.org/github/moj-analytical-services/splink_demos/blob/max_pair/04_Estimating_model_parameters.ipynb)).

The main failure mode is chained over-merging at colonies: A looks like B and B looks like C, so all three get merged even though A and C are different cats. Two defenses apply. Each new link is checked pairwise against the chain's representative photos. And merges are reversible: a merge writes a `ChainMerged` event and redirects the absorbed chain's URL instead of deleting it.

Clustering is a different job from identity. ST-DBSCAN, which groups points that are close in both space and time, can run over unlinked sightings and propose new colony or community-dog chains ([stdbscan](https://archive.linux.duke.edu/cran/web/packages/stdbscan/readme/README.html)). The space-time permutation scan statistic finds unusual bursts using only case counts ([Kulldorff et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC548794)), and New York City runs it daily for disease surveillance ([CDC EID](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/22/10/16-0097)). Here it can flag a burst of suspected poisonings in one bairro for NGOs and authorities.

Time needs two axes. *Valid time* is when the animal was seen, stored as an interval with a precision flag. *Transaction time* is when the platform recorded the report. With both, late reports slot into the past without rewriting history, corrections become new events, and moderators can ask what the map showed on a given date. Fuzzy phrases such as "ontem à tarde" become an interval (yesterday 12:00–18:00), and the original text is kept, following the "approximate" and "uncertain" qualifiers of the Library of Congress EDTF standard ([LoC EDTF](https://www.loc.gov/standards/datetime/)).

Exact geometry lives in a PostGIS `geography` column, used for radius queries. H3 cells, 64-bit integers that tile the map in near-uniform hexagons ([H3](https://h3geo.org/docs/core-library/restable/)), handle candidate lookup and display at three sizes. Resolution 9 (about 0.105 km² per cell) narrows down candidate matches around the link radii for cats and community dogs. Resolution 8 (about 0.737 km²) is the default public "approximate" cell, and resolution 7 (about 5.16 km²) is used for sensitive places.

Candidates are first gathered from a neighborhood of H3 cells, then filtered exactly with `ST_DWithin`, because hexagon edges create boundary effects. The key invariant is that observations exist independently of chains, and chain membership is only ever expressed through append-only link rows. Storing observations inside chains would make merges and splits destructive.

### Three clocks: location freshness, case liveness and emergency urgency

Location freshness decays as f = 2^(−Δt/h), using the half-lives h from the table. A case with f of 0.5 or more shows as active. Between 0.5 and 0.125 it shows as *desatualizado*: the marker fades, and a one-tap "Ainda está aí?" prompt goes to the reporter and to recent viewers nearby. Below 0.125 the case drops off the default map layer but remains searchable.

"Última vez visto" is computed only from confirmed links and shown at its true precision: "visto ontem à tarde," never "às 15:00." Unconfirmed suggestions appear as "possível avistamento" and never move the marker. A "não está mais" answer is valuable negative evidence. It should cut freshness immediately and, for lost pets, widen the search radius.

Case liveness is the second clock. It follows the dormancy column: lost pets go dormant at 60 or 90 days, emergencies are archived after 72 hours without an update, and community animals never close, only asking for yearly re-confirmation.

Urgency is the third clock. The Veterinary Triage List, validated on 485 dogs and cats, sets target waiting times of 0 minutes (red), 15 (orange), 30–60 (yellow) and 120 (green). It agreed with a panel of veterinarians on 95.5% of red cases ([PubMed 22702436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22702436/)). Lay reporters cannot diagnose. The platform should instead ask observable yes/no questions in the style of START, the triage method used in mass-casualty incidents, and map the answers to colors. Is it breathing? Can it stand? Is it bleeding heavily? Is it trapped? Is it foaming at the mouth or trembling?

A red case with no "estou indo" within its target time widens the alert ring and pages partner clinics, NGOs or the CCZ. These lay questions are untested, and Brazilian veterinarians need to validate them before launch.

### Location privacy needs access control, because noise destroys search

The platform faces a hard tension: the radius that protects a feeder's home or a colony is about the same size as the radius needed to find the animal. Geo-indistinguishability, the standard way to apply differential privacy to locations, adds random noise drawn from a planar Laplace distribution ([Andrés et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1984v1)). Under that distribution, protecting a point at privacy level ln 4 within 200 m implies an **average displacement of about 289 m, and 684 m at the 95th percentile**. That is far beyond the 50 m median at which lost cats are found. Noise therefore belongs only in public aggregate layers; precision for the people actually searching must come from access control.

Noise also adds up. A feeder who reports the same colony daily, with fresh random noise each time, leaks its true location within weeks, because averaging the noisy points recovers it. Fixed sensitive places therefore get one stable masked representation: an H3 cell, or a single offset point seeded by an HMAC of the place's ID.

Donut masking, which moves each point at least a minimum distance and at most a maximum, assumes uniform population density and under-protects where density varies ([Allshouse et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2984253)). That describes a Brazilian city, where a favela, a condomínio and an industrial zone sit side by side.

iNaturalist's experience adds two leak paths to close: obscured locations can be inferred from the same user's nearby open observations, and coarsened public dates leaked through exact dates in its API ([iNaturalist](https://help.inaturalist.org/support/solutions/articles/151000169938)). Alerta Patinha should therefore have no public "reports by this user" map. No API response should carry more precision than the viewer's tier allows. And users should be able to declare *zonas privadas* (home, feeding point): observations inside one are automatically coarsened and shown only to the day.

| Viewer | Emergency in a public place | Lost-pet sighting | Community animal or colony | Reporter's home zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anonymous visitor | Exact pin while active; no reporter identity; time to the hour | H3 res 8 cell; time to the day | H3 res 7 cell, or hidden | Never shown |
| Signed-in user nearby | Exact | H3 res 9 cell | H3 res 7 cell | Never shown |
| Chain member or verified helper | Exact, plus relayed chat with the reporter | Exact | Exact only with the caretaker's approval, logged | Never; meet at a public point |
| Moderator or partner NGO | Exact, logged | Exact, logged | Exact, logged | Exact, logged, limited to a stated purpose |

The one deliberate exception to coarse-by-default is an active emergency in a public place, because a passerby without an account is the likeliest rescuer. That trades some risk of scraping for speed. The exception does not apply to categories hidden automatically (valuable breeds, colonies, home zones). Reporters always choose "exato" or "aproximado" explicitly, and the exact pin snaps to a coarse cell once the case is resolved.

How the map draws uncertainty also matters. When people judged a location from a "blue dot," a uniform circle with a border produced the most accurate and fastest judgments ([McKenzie et al. 2016](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/assessing-the-effectiveness-of-different-visualizations-for-judgm/)). People also tend to assume the true location is at the center of an uncertainty region ([Hegarty et al.](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/44b4f5md)). So exact points are pins with a stem ("local exato"), GPS uncertainty is a bordered translucent circle, and privacy masking is a bordered hexagon with a lock, both shown without a center dot.

What the map loads depends on zoom. At country and state zoom, the server sends counts per H3 cell. At city zoom, it sends active, non-sensitive items, and the browser clusters them with Supercluster, which handled 400,000 points at 60 frames per second in Mapbox's demo ([Mapbox](https://blog.mapbox.com/clustering-millions-of-points-on-a-map-with-supercluster-272046ec5c97)). At street zoom, masked items never resolve below their cell. Heatmaps belong to analytics, such as abandonment hotspots or targeting castration campaigns. They should be capped at H3 resolution 7, with cells holding fewer than three items suppressed, since a fine-grained colony heatmap is itself a leak.

The invariant that ties all of this together: masking happens on the server before data is sent, so no client ever receives coordinates more precise than its viewer's tier allows.

## Photos, re-identification and WhatsApp intake turn messy inputs into evidence

### The photo pipeline strips, derives and never serves originals

Every uploaded photo carries a privacy hazard (GPS in its EXIF metadata, faces, house numbers, license plates) and a piece of evidence (when and where it was taken). libvips handles both cheaply. It completed the reference load-crop-shrink-sharpen-save benchmark in 0.69 s using 109 MB of memory, against Pillow-SIMD's 1.51 s and 1,040 MB ([libvips](https://github.com/libvips/libvips/wiki/Speed-and-memory-use)).

Ingest starts by checking the file's magic bytes to confirm its real format, then decodes it (including HEIC). It copies the EXIF time and GPS into a private evidence row and rotates the image to its correct orientation. It then writes metadata-stripped WebP copies: about 1600 px for the detail view, about 640 px for cards, a 256–384 px crop for embeddings, and a tiny ThumbHash placeholder. The original stays in a private bucket with short retention.

EXIF is useful when present but cannot be trusted. WhatsApp typically re-encodes media and drops it (expected, but not yet tested), and screenshots and edited images carry misleading values.

Meta's PDQ perceptual hash, starting with a threshold of Hamming distance 31 or less, links reposts of the same photo ([PDQ](https://github.com/facebook/ThreatExchange/blob/main/pdq/README.md)). Reposts are common when one injured-dog image circulates through neighborhood groups. PDQ does not handle rotation, so each rotation should be hashed too. It also says nothing about whether two different photos show the same animal.

Meta's EgoBlur face and license-plate detectors are released under Apache 2.0 ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/projectaria/EgoBlur)). They should run on every public copy, with the threshold set to catch as much as possible: blurring part of a wall by mistake is harmless, while a missed face is a privacy incident. Graphic injuries are best flagged by adding a `graphic_injury` field to the same vision-model call that extracts species and coat. When that field is uncertain, the image defaults to blurred behind a tap.

Licensing is the trap in detection. Ultralytics YOLO weights are AGPL-3.0, which can oblige a networked service to release its source code. RF-DETR is Apache 2.0 ([Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/compare/yolo26-vs-rf-detr)), and MegaDetector v6 ships MIT and Apache variants ([Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/Biodiversity/releases/past_releases/)).

On the phone, downscaling to about 2048 px before upload cuts a 3–5 MB photo to a few hundred KB, the single biggest data saving for prepaid users. The invariant: nothing public is ever served from an original file.

### Re-identification ranks candidates; people decide

Published accuracy comes from easy data. PetFace (ECCV 2024) covers 257,484 individual animals ([PetFace](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13555v2)). It reports a dog verification AUC of 99.45%, measuring whether two photos show the same animal. On re-identification, picking the right animal from a gallery, the first guess is right **77.86% of the time for dogs and 54.29% for cats**. Those results use 224×224 face crops aligned on facial landmarks and sourced from pet-shop and adoption sites. Commercial claims of 98–99% (Petnow's nose prints, a frontal-face system in Baltimore) are company figures with no published protocol or audit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/20/petnow-claims-to-be-able-to-identify-dogs-and-cats-from-their-snouts); [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/products/facial-recognition-to-reconnect-pets-owners-in-baltimore.html)).

Street photos of vira-latas differ on every axis. Poses are not frontal and the whole body is in frame. Animals are photographed at a distance, at night or in motion. Huge numbers of caramelo and black dogs look nearly identical. And owners' reference photos are often selfies. No benchmark exists for this kind of data, and the realistic expectation is first-guess accuracy well below PetFace's.

The design target is therefore recall: **the true match appears on the first screen of 10–20 candidates**. Filtering hard by species, radius and time window first shrinks the pool from millions to tens or hundreds. In Postgres, that means an exact similarity scan over the filtered rows rather than an approximate index. pgvector's HNSW index applies filters after scanning, so at default settings a filter matching 10% of rows returns only about 4 results unless iterative scans are enabled ([pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector)). HNSW with iterative scan is therefore reserved for wide-radius searches. The re-identification score then enters chain linking as one vote with capped weight, never as an automatic merge.

Licensing shapes the choice of model. MegaDescriptor beats CLIP and DINOv2 on animal re-identification benchmarks ([WACV 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/html/Cermak_WildlifeDatasets_An_Open-Source_Toolkit_for_Animal_Re-Identification_WACV_2024_paper.html)). But it is licensed CC-BY-NC-4.0, non-commercial only ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/BVRA/MegaDescriptor-L-384)), which is risky for anything with sponsors or revenue. DINOv3 carries a custom license that needs legal review ([Meta](https://ai.meta.com/resources/models-and-libraries/dinov3-license)).

PetFace's own results show where the real gains come from. A model trained for the task with ArcFace reached 99.45% verification AUC on dogs, against 91.86% for general-purpose CLIP. The path that follows is to start with a general model under a permissive license, used as it is (DINOv2 is Apache 2.0), and to log every human "sim/não" decision as a labeled pair. Once thousands of confirmed pairs of Brazilian SRD animals exist, an ArcFace head can be fine-tuned on them.

The cold start favors this path. On day one there is no gallery of lost pets, so the first value is linking sightings to sightings. Next comes owners uploading three to five photos of their pet (side, front, face). Store the model name and version with each embedding, because embeddings from different models are not comparable. Nose-print biometrics need a cooperative close-up and are irrelevant for strays.

### The WhatsApp agent is a parser inside a deterministic dialogue

The agent must use Meta's official Cloud API. The On-Premises API stopped sending messages on 23 October 2025 ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/on-premises/sunset)). Unofficial libraries that emulate WhatsApp Web, such as Baileys and Evolution API, violate the terms. Vendor reports say they risk number bans, with opaque appeals ([Zylos](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-01-26-whatsapp-api-automation)).

Messages from users are free, and each one opens a 24-hour service window in which the platform can reply freely. Two changes took effect on **1 October 2026**: service replies beyond 1,000 per month per business number are now charged, and utility templates sent inside an open window are no longer free ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). Meta gives Brazil's rate for utility and authentication messages as **about US$0.0068 per message**. Third-party guides put marketing messages at about US$0.0625. Brazil's rate for service replies beyond the free 1,000 was not retrieved, and it is the largest unknown in the cost per report. Accounts must migrate to billing in reais by 30 June 2027 ([Meta pricing.md](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md)). Case updates sent after the 24-hour window should use utility templates, never marketing ones.

Meta's terms bar AI providers from the platform when AI is "a funcionalidade principal (em vez de incidental ou auxiliar)," at Meta's sole discretion ([Meta Terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)). A bot built for one task, taking in animal reports, sits on the incidental side. Brazil adds two further protections. CADE, the competition authority, unanimously upheld a preventive measure on 4 March 2026 suspending the restriction, backed by a daily fine of R$250,000 ([Mercado&Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/04/03/2026/noticias/cade-mantem-medida-preventiva-que-obriga-meta-a-permitir-chatbots-de-ia-no-whatsapp/)). And Meta now admits AI providers on +55 numbers at its published rates ([Meta AI Providers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers/)). Keeping the bot narrowly scoped keeps it outside the ban however the CADE inquiry ends: fixed refusals for off-topic chat, no open conversation with the LLM, and a documented purpose. The same clause restricts using platform data to train AI models in some setups, so training re-identification on photos received through WhatsApp needs legal review first.

The dependable design treats the LLM as a parser, not the thing running the conversation. Each turn, a small model fills a `Report` object constrained to a JSON schema; Anthropic's structured outputs enforce this ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). The fields cover kind, species and count; coat colors in everyday terms such as caramelo, tigrado or frajola; size; condition from a fixed list including atropelado, sarna and bicheira; urgency; location text and landmarks; a structured time expression; and a normalized summary. Every field can be empty and carries a confidence score.

Code, not the model, runs the dialogue. It works out which critical field is missing (species, location and kind, plus condition for emergencies) and asks at most one or two pre-written PT-BR questions per turn. After two failed attempts it stops and files the report as incomplete. It flags contradictions ("Na foto parece um cachorro, é isso mesmo?"). It resolves relative time against the message timestamp in America/Sao_Paulo, so "agora há pouco" becomes an interval covering the last half hour. For location it prefers a WhatsApp location pin. Free text such as "em frente ao posto Ipiranga da av. Brasil" is geocoded with a bias toward the user's area and read back for confirmation, and a coordinate guessed by the LLM is never published without that confirmation.

Prompt injection is contained because the model has no tools that can change anything, every field is validated with Pydantic, and CPF and phone-number patterns are stripped from public fields. The worst case is then a malformed report, not a harmful reply.

At these prices, the language work is cheap. Claude Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), and gpt-5-nano and Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite sit in the same band ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); [Google](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). That puts a four-turn report with two photos at about **US$2 per 1,000 reports**, against roughly US$35–45 with a Sonnet-class model. The larger model should be used only after repeated schema failures.

Voice notes are common on WhatsApp. Transcription costs $0.003 per minute with gpt-4o-mini-transcribe ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)), about $1.50 per 1,000 thirty-second notes. Accuracy is the concern. Whisper got 14.5% of words wrong on spontaneous Brazilian interviews, and the best model on spontaneous speech from São Paulo got 24.2% wrong ([BRACIS 2024](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/bracis/article/view/33550)). The pipeline should therefore feed the transcriber domain vocabulary (vira-lata, bicheira, local bairros) and always confirm the parsed location. Self-hosted faster-whisper transcribes 13 minutes of audio in 1 minute 42 seconds with its small model on an eight-thread CPU ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). Self-hosting is therefore a privacy decision rather than a cost decision, until volume is very high.

All in, the variable costs of AI and messaging come to roughly **US$15–25 per 1,000 reports**, before the unknown service-message charges. WhatsApp follow-ups dominate that figure, not AI: two utility templates per report account for about $13.60 of it. Quality should be measured on a gold set of 300–500 real, anonymized messages containing misspellings, gírias, regional terms and transcripts. Track per-field precision and recall, how well the extracted time intervals overlap the true ones, geocoding error in meters, and the number of questions asked per completed report.

## Keep FastAPI, Postgres and Next.js, and change what surrounds them

No evidence justifies switching frameworks. FastAPI (0.159.0) with SQLAlchemy 2.1 async, GeoAlchemy2 and Alembic gives typed access to PostGIS, and the team already runs it with import-linter enforcing module boundaries. Django's real advantages are its admin and GeoDjango. But Django 6.0's new tasks framework ships with no production worker ([Django 6.0](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.0/releases/6.0/)), Django 6.1 lists no async ORM improvements ([Django 6.1](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.1/releases/6.1/)), and moderation here is so map- and photo-centric that it needs a custom Next.js back office anyway, with sqladmin for internal CRUD. Two FastAPI traps deserve naming. Its WebSocket docs point multi-process users to `encode/broadcaster`, which is archived ([FastAPI](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/advanced/websockets/); [GitHub](https://github.com/encode/broadcaster)). And fastapi-users is in maintenance mode ([GitHub](https://github.com/fastapi-users/fastapi-users)). The governing invariant is that one Python backend owns every write and every authorization decision; realtime, tiles and workers only read downstream or execute jobs.

The database is PostgreSQL 18 (18.6) with PostGIS 3.6.4, pgvector 0.8.7 and h3-pg. Two Docker gotchas break copy-pasted Compose files. The official `postgis/postgis:18-3.6` image is amd64-only ([docker-postgis](https://github.com/postgis/docker-postgis)), which matters on ARM hosts such as AWS Graviton or Oracle's free Ampere tier. And the PG18 image moved its data volume to `/var/lib/postgresql` ([docker-library](https://github.com/docker-library/postgres/blob/master/18/trixie/Dockerfile)), so a Compose file with the old mount path silently starts an empty cluster. h3-pg moved to `postgis/h3-pg` (4.5.0), while the PGDG package for PG18 is still 4.2.3 ([postgis/h3-pg](https://github.com/postgis/h3-pg)), so the package version should be pinned. PostgreSQL 19 reaches general availability on 19 November 2026 ([PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-19-rc-1-released-3409/)) and PostGIS 3.7 is still a release candidate, so neither belongs in a launch. Sightings and chain events should be partitioned by observation month from day one. Search in Brazilian Portuguese needs a custom configuration combining the Portuguese stemmer with `unaccent`, plus `pg_trgm` for fuzzy matching on pet and street names.

Per-chain chat and live map updates belong in **Centrifugo 6 running on Valkey**. Centrifugo (Apache-2.0) provides message history with automatic recovery on reconnect, presence, and a fallback to server-sent events ([Centrifugo](https://centrifugal.dev/docs/getting-started/introduction)). Valkey 9.1 is BSD-3, while Redis 8 moved to a triple license of RSALv2, SSPL and AGPL ([Redis](https://github.com/redis/redis/blob/unstable/LICENSE.txt)). Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY should not be used to fan out messages: committing a transaction that issued NOTIFY takes a global lock that serialized commits and caused outages at Recall.ai ([Recall.ai](https://recall.ai/blog/postgres-listen-notify-does-not-scale)), and the fix landed only in the PostgreSQL 19 line ([commit 282b1cde](https://github.com/postgres/postgres/commit/282b1cde9dedf456ecf02eb27caf086023a7bb71)). Messages are saved to Postgres first and published after the commit, so Centrifugo is a buffer for reconnecting clients, never the record.

Background work goes to **procrastinate**, an async job queue stored in Postgres. It enqueues each job in the same transaction as the write that caused it ([procrastinate](https://github.com/procrastinate-org/procrastinate/releases)), which removes the bug where a report exists but its photo job was lost. arq is in maintenance-only mode ([arq](https://github.com/python-arq/arq)), and Celery is built around synchronous code. The CPU-heavy image pipeline runs in its own worker container. Deciding who gets notified about a new case can start as a spatial `ST_Intersects` query (GiST-indexed) against each subscriber's watch area, stored as a buffer around their chosen point. When subscriber counts demand it, this can move to an H3 `watch_cells` table matched by simple equality, which reuses the same cell keys as Centrifugo's map channels. Delivery is Web Push over VAPID.

The map is MapLibre GL JS 6, reading a self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles file of Brazil from object storage behind a CDN. MapLibre fetches it with HTTP range requests, so no tile server is needed. The arithmetic is decisive. Google Dynamic Maps at about 10 million loads a month would cost roughly **US$16,000/month** at published tiers ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). The OpenStreetMap Foundation's tile servers offer no SLA and may withdraw access ([OSMF](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/)). The free tiers of Stadia and MapTiler exclude commercial use or pause service over quota ([Stadia](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/); [MapTiler](https://www.maptiler.com/cloud/pricing/)). OpenFreeMap works as a free default during development and as a fallback, though it has no SLA either ([OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/)).

MapLibre 6 requires WebGL2 ([MapLibre](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/releases/tag/v6.0.0)), so low-end Android phones need a list view to fall back on when the map fails to load. The platform's own map data starts as a GeoJSON endpoint that takes a bounding box and clusters points on the server. It graduates to vector tiles served by Martin from PostGIS functions ([Martin](https://github.com/maplibre/martin)); the alternative, pg_tileserv, has not released since February 2024. Geocoding later moves to self-hosted Photon and Nominatim.

The client is a Next.js 16 progressive web app (PWA). On Android, with about 78% of the market, it gets install prompts, Web Push and Background Sync. On iOS, push notifications work only after the user adds the app to the home screen ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/progressive-web-apps)), and Background Sync does not exist in Safari ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/background-sync)). The camera opens through a plain `<input type="file" accept="image/*" capture="environment">` with no permission prompt. Location is requested only after the user taps "usar minha localização," with dropping a pin on the map as the fallback. The user chooses "exato" or "aproximado" when submitting.

Every report is first written to an IndexedDB outbox on the device with a client-generated UUIDv7. The server enforces that ID as an idempotency key, because offline retries would otherwise create duplicates. Serwist is the service-worker library the Next.js documentation points to ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/progressive-web-apps)). Performance needs a budget. Alex Russell's 2023 estimate for serving typical (75th-percentile) devices worldwide was about 300–350 KiB of gzipped JavaScript in total ([Infrequently Noted](https://infrequently.org/series/performance-inequality)). The emergency report screen should work without the map and stay well under that budget, with MapLibre loaded only on map screens. A native app built with Expo is worth adding only if iOS push opt-in or background location becomes what limits growth.

MinIO's community edition should not be adopted: its repository is archived and no binaries have shipped since October 2025 ([GitHub](https://github.com/minio/minio)). Garage or SeaweedFS provide S3-compatible storage in development. For production media, Cloudflare R2 charges nothing for egress, but it has no South American location or jurisdiction ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/)). Using it is therefore an international data transfer that needs the ANPD's standard contractual clauses ([Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/unico/regulamentacao-transferencia-internacional-dados/)); Magalu Cloud is the in-country alternative. Uploads go through a presigned URL into a private quarantine bucket and are served only after a worker re-encodes them.

Authentication is anonymous-first. A device session stored on the server can report immediately. Upgrading to a phone-verified identity uses WhatsApp authentication templates at about US$0.0068 each, versus Twilio SMS at US$0.0599 per segment ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/br)). For 100,000 verifications a month, that is roughly US$884 against US$7,790. Opaque, revocable session tokens stored in Postgres or Valkey are enough for a single backend. Keycloak or Zitadel (now AGPL) earn their weight only if partner NGOs need single sign-on.

The database, API, backups and logs containing personal data stay in São Paulo. There, AWS on-demand instances cost about 60% more than in Virginia ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/on-demand/)). A single-VM MVP costs between US$0 on Oracle's free ARM tier ([Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/resourceref.htm)) and about US$190/month on an AWS m7g.xlarge. At about one million monthly users, the estimate is US$3–6k/month at on-demand prices. Most of that is the database and media egress, and a CDN or R2 removes most of the egress cost. Errors and traces go through Sentry, with personal data scrubbed, and OpenTelemetry. Backups use pgBackRest 2.60, upgraded past the October 2026 weak-encryption advisory, with a restore test every month ([pgBackRest](https://github.com/pgbackrest/pgbackrest)).

| Compose service | Component (version, Oct 2026) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `edge` | Caddy | TLS, routing, WebSocket proxy |
| `web` | Next.js 16.4 PWA with Serwist | Public map, report flow, moderation back office |
| `api` | FastAPI 0.159, SQLAlchemy 2.1, GeoAlchemy2 | Sole owner of writes and authorization |
| `worker` | procrastinate 3.10, pyvips | Photo pipeline, LLM and transcription calls, notification fan-out |
| `realtime` | Centrifugo 6.10 | Per-chain chat and live map channels |
| `cache` | Valkey 9.1 | Centrifugo engine, rate limits, sessions |
| `db` | PostgreSQL 18.6, PostGIS 3.6.4, pgvector 0.8.7, h3-pg | Single source of truth |
| `s3` (development) | Garage or SeaweedFS | S3-compatible media store; R2 or Magalu in production |
| Added later | Martin, Photon, Nominatim | Vector tiles and self-hosted geocoding |

The deployment grows in stages. It starts on a single VM. At roughly 50,000–300,000 monthly users, the database moves to its own host with a streaming replica, Martin takes over map tiles, notification matching moves to H3 cells, and an embedding worker is added. Near one million monthly users, Centrifugo becomes a cluster, the notification fan-out moves to Valkey, and all media goes through a CDN. Kubernetes is not worth the overhead before that point.

Four invariants hold at every stage. Postgres is the only source of truth. Approximate geometry is derived when the data is written, and it is the only geometry ever sent publicly. Every client write is idempotent. And uploaded bytes are never served before they have been re-encoded.

## Alerta Patinha is free to claim and needs defensive filing now

The owner's original candidates were both blocked. Pending INPI applications for "cuidapet" cover classes 09 and 42, the software and IT-services classes the platform needs. EncontraPet launched in 2026 as both a live lost-pet website and a Google Play app ([encontrapet.com.br](https://encontrapet.com.br/)).

Of 38 names checked live on 21 October 2026, Alerta Patinha tied for second place with Alerta Bicho and SOS Patinha, each scoring 35 out of 45. Its strengths fit the product. "Alerta" covers both jobs the platform depends on, an emergency and a casual sighting, whereas "SOS" frames every post as an emergency and discourages the low-stakes sightings that build chains. "Patinha" is spelled exactly as it is heard. "Bicho," by contrast, is also written "bixo," and app-store searches for "bicho" names are flooded with jogo-do-bicho gambling apps and insect apps ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=alerta%20bicho&c=apps&hl=pt_BR&gl=BR)). A "bicho" label would also invite off-scope reports of scorpions and rats. Availability is clean: alertapatinha and alertapatinhas both came back unregistered on .com.br, .org.br, .app.br and .com ([registro.br RDAP](https://rdap.registro.br/domain/alertapatinha.com.br); [Verisign RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/alertapatinha.com)). None of INPI's 332 trademark processes containing "patinha" is an alert or SOS mark ([INPI pePI](https://busca.inpi.gov.br/pePI/jsp/marcas/Pesquisa_classe_basica.jsp)).

The choice has three costs. At 13 characters, alertapatinha is the longest domain on the shortlist. "Patinha" leans toward dogs and cats: that covers the core use, but it weakens reports of horses or birds. And the name sits in a crowded space. Many protection NGOs use "Patinhas," such as Patinhas Carentes in Espírito Santo and Patinhas Slz in Maranhão ([A Gazeta](https://www.agazeta.com.br/hz/pet/em-dificuldades-ong-do-es-que-cuida-de-mais-de-100-animais-busca-novo-espaco-0424); [O Imparcial](https://oimparcial.com.br/cidades/2019/12/5-ongs-para-adotar-animais-de-estimacao-em-sao-luis/5/)), as do several pet-care apps ([App Store search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?country=br&entity=software&limit=6&term=patinha)). It therefore risks being read as one more local group rather than a national civic platform. On Google Play it would also sit near "Alerta Animal," a wildlife roadkill app ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=br.com.alerta.animal.rs)).

A domain that comes back unregistered is a strong signal, not a guarantee. INPI awards marks to whoever files first, and several same-concept apps launched in 2026. The first step is to register both spellings on all four domains and redirect them to the main site. Next, a trademark attorney should run a phonetic search before filing at INPI in classes 09, 42 and 45. The Instagram handles need a manual check, since the research could not see past Instagram's login wall. Store listings should carry a descriptive subtitle such as "animais perdidos, vistos e feridos." The cheapest available experiment is also worth running: dictate the name in a WhatsApp voice note to low-literacy users, check what they type, and measure how often the singular and plural get confused. A caramelo mascot would capture the national icon of the vira-lata without making the brand dog-only.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what kind of project Alerta Patinha is. Its hard problems are not the map or the AI. Both are cheap and mature in October 2026: a full multi-turn report costs about a fifth of a cent in model tokens. The hard problems are institutional. The first is closing loops reliably in a country where only one in five people trusts local government. The second is routing work without burning out the small, mostly female core of protetores who carry it. The third is publishing enough to mobilize strangers without handing scammers, poisoners and thieves a targeting tool. That makes the platform's most valuable long-term asset the labeled record its own operation produces: every confirmed or rejected link, every "ainda está lá," every resolution. Those labels recalibrate the radii and half-lives borrowed from other countries. They train a matching model for Brazilian street animals that nobody else has. And once deduplicated, they produce the first continuous map of where Brazil's street animals are. That map is exactly the asset that could turn a CCZ or the Ministry of the Environment from a distant stakeholder into a partner, the kind of anchoring that made Petco Love Lost the default abroad.

Sequencing follows from this. The first release should bet on four things: WhatsApp intake, fast closure with photos for first-time reporters, ring alerting with claims that expire, and enforcing location precision on the server. Re-identification, vector tiles and H3-based notification matching can wait for data and scale. The guardrail metrics should avoid Citizen's trap. They are the second-report rate among first-time reporters, the funnel from alert to acceptance to arrival, time to first response, and how concentrated caseload is across protetores. Publish volume and engagement should never be targets. The open questions are cheap to answer and should be scheduled rather than assumed: Brazil's WhatsApp rate for service messages, re-identification recall on real street photos, veterinary validation of the lay triage questions, alert radii for each city, the name dictation test, and legal review of the WhatsApp data-use clause, the STF ruling and the ECA Digital.

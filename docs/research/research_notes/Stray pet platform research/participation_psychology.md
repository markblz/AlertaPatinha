# Psychology & Behavioral Economics of Citizen Participation in Civic Crowdsourcing and Animal-Welfare Helping (for a Brazilian stray/lost-pet platform)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Scope: what makes people start contributing, keep contributing, and actually act, plus design implications. Evidence-strength flags used throughout: **[STRONG]** (large-N field data / RCT / robust meta-analysis), **[MODERATE]**, **[WEAK]** (small-N, single study, industry/vendor, secondary summary only), **[CONTESTED]** (replication or publication-bias problems), **[CLASSIC]** (pre-2010).

---

## 1. What drives and sustains contribution in crowdsourced civic / citizen-science / VGI platforms?

### Takeaway
Contribution is extremely concentrated: most people contribute once, a small core (≈10% of users) does ≈80% of the work, and retention past the first year is low (≈15–40% return depending on platform). What sustains people is feedback that their contribution mattered, early welcoming socialization (not rejection), and symbolic, peer-given recognition. Points, badges, leaderboards and streaks produce small, uneven, and sometimes negative effects. Directly contingent rewards push quantity over quality.

### Cited Findings

**Participation inequality / long tail**
- Zooniverse (7 projects, 180 days): 100,386 participants contributed 129,500 hours of unpaid labor (valued at more than US$1.5M). Most contributors participate only once and with little effort, so a relatively small share of returning users does most of the work. Top contributors got there mainly through more effort, and they also worked faster, with the speed gap growing across sessions (learning). Activity tends to decline, with spikes from outreach or media attention. **[STRONG]** — [Sauermann & Franzoni 2015, PNAS (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4311847); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/01/150105151046.htm)
- In the same study the top 10% of contributors did "almost 80% of the work on average" (up to 88% in one project). The share of users who returned for a second time ranged from 17% to 40% (average 27%), and mean daily time per contributor was 7.18–26.23 minutes. **[STRONG; figures via secondary summary]** — [Journalist's Resource summary of Sauermann & Franzoni](https://journalistsresource.org/?p=44784)
- OpenStreetMap: in April 2009 only 33,440 of 120,000 registered users had made at least one edit. Neis & Zipf (2012) describe OSM contributions as fitting the 90-9-1 participation-inequality pattern. **[MODERATE]** — [Neis & Zipf 2012, ISPRS IJGI](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijgi/ijgi-01-00146/article_deploy/ijgi-01-00146.pdf). A secondary summary of the same paper says only 38% of registered members had ever edited by end-2011 and only 5% (~24,000) actively contributed. **[WEAK, secondary, verify in the paper]** — [OSM diary (PierZen)](https://api.openstreetmap.org/user/PierZen/diary/42470)
- OSM contribution inequality gets *worse* over time in countries without large data imports. **[MODERATE]** — [Yang, Fan, Sun & Zipf 2016 record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1539759393); context: >3.6M registered OSM contributors by April 2017, with documented strong inequality — [Leeds GeoComputation 2017 paper](https://www.geog.leeds.ac.uk/groups/geocomp/2017/papers/64.pdf)
- iNaturalist City Nature Challenge (San Francisco): about 15–20% of 2019 newcomers were still participating in 2020. Users who were successfully onboarded went on to record more observations. **[MODERATE, preprint]** — [arXiv 2112.02693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02693)
- iNaturalistUK (4.8M UK observations): most users contribute only in their first year, and long-term retention is low. **[MODERATE]** — [NBN Trust analysis of iNaturalistUK records (2025)](https://nbn.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Analysis-of-iNaturalistUK-Observation-Records-Revised-version-2.pdf)
- Young bioblitz volunteers on iNaturalist: steady, regular contribution was associated with more active days, and long time on the platform was negatively associated with contribution. **[MODERATE]** — [Aristeidou et al. 2021, PLOS ONE (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815142)
- "Dabblers" are the majority. In Old Weather (Zooniverse), high contributors were strongly drawn by social or competitive features, while low contributors described solitary, short bursts of activity. The authors recommend designing for lone, low-commitment workers ("just another page"), splitting work into low-effort chunks, giving feedback on the quality and value of each contribution, and trying to win back early drop-outs. **[MODERATE, survey + interviews]** — [Eveleigh et al. 2014, CHI (UCL Discovery)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1418573)

**Newcomer socialization**
- Wikipedia's decline: the proportion of "desirable" newcomers did not change after 2006, but the share reverted (rejected) in their first session rose. The decline is tied to newcomer rejection and the spread of bots and algorithmic quality-control tools. **[STRONG]** — [Halfaker, Geiger, Morgan & Riedl 2013 (PDF)](https://stuartgeiger.com/papers/abs-rise-and-decline-wikipedia.pdf). A replication across 769 Wikia wikis found the same dynamic on the average wiki: newcomers were reverted more and kept editing less. **[STRONG]** — [Community Data Science Collective](https://blog.communitydata.science/revisiting-the-rise-and-decline/)
- Zooniverse newcomer work uses Legitimate Peripheral Participation (LPP) theory: people move from the periphery toward the core, and platform features can act as motivators toward sustained participation. A CSCW 2016 paper traced 1,687 newcomers' movement through a project. **[MODERATE; full findings not retrieved]** — [Jackson, Østerlund et al. (Syracuse bibliography)](https://floss.syr.edu/bibcite/export/bibtex/bibcite_reference/6829)
- iNaturalist staff explicitly recommend that identifiers filter for observations from accounts created in the last day or week so that new users get fast responses. This is practitioner guidance, not an experiment. **[WEAK]** — [iNaturalist Australia forum post](https://inaturalist.ala.org.au/posts/23794-tips-and-tricks-for-welcoming-helping-new-users)

**Recognition and rewards (field experiments)**
- Wikipedia natural field experiment: randomized, purely symbolic awards (no material value, visible only on pseudonymous profiles) raised newcomer retention, and the effect lasted four quarters. Proposed mechanisms are identification with the community, status and reputation, recognition, and self-confidence. **[STRONG]** — [Gallus 2017, Management Science (open access)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2540). A secondary summary puts the gain at about 20% higher retention in the following month. **[WEAK, verify]** — [Economic Rockstar interview](https://economicrockstar.com/?p=1826)
- Wikipedia "barnstar" RCT: of 200 top-1% editors who had never received an award, 100 were given one. Recipients were about 60% more productive over the next 90 days and six times more likely to receive further barnstars from others (a virtuous cycle). **[STRONG, but n=200 and top editors only]** — [Restivo & van de Rijt 2012, PLOS ONE (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315525)
- Stack Overflow badges: a formal model plus data show that badges can steer behavior, both increasing participation and shifting the activity mix. **[MODERATE]** — [Anderson, Huttenlocher, Kleinberg & Leskovec 2013, WWW](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2013/proceedings/p95.pdf). A re-analysis estimates that only about 20% of users actually respond to badge incentives (a "phantom steering effect"). **[MODERATE]** — [Hoernle et al. 2020 (arXiv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.06160). Another study puts it at about 5–30% of users who earn badges. — [Springer KAIS 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10115-021-01637-6)

**Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation / crowding-out**
- 40-year meta-analysis (k = 183, N = 212,468): intrinsic motivation is a medium-to-strong predictor of performance (ρ = .21–.45). Intrinsic motivation predicts *quality* better, while incentives predict *quantity* better. When incentives are directly tied to performance, intrinsic motivation matters less (a crowding-out pattern); when the link is indirect, it matters more. The two are "not necessarily antagonistic." **[STRONG]** — [Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford 2014, Psychological Bulletin](https://oa.mg/work/10.1037/a0035661); [Science for Work summary](https://scienceforwork.com/blog/incentives-motivation-performance/)

**Gamification evidence**
- Programming education, multi-level meta-analysis: overall effect d = 0.4, driven mainly by *levels*. **Badges, points, avatars and leaderboards had no significant effect** on learning outcomes. **[MODERATE; education domain]** — [ISCTE 2023 meta-analysis](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/29213/1/article_95533.pdf)
- Gamification in formal education: medium pooled effects, g = .574 on affective outcomes and g = .740 on behavioral outcomes (13 behavioral and 19 affective studies), with 14 design elements tested as moderators. **[MODERATE; small k, education domain]** — [ECNU-indexed meta-analysis](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/zh/publications/a-meta-analysis-on-the-influence-of-gamification-in-formal-educat/)
- Asynchronous online learning (2026): motivation g = 0.52. **Narrative-based gamification beat points/badges and leaderboards.** **[MODERATE; education domain]** — [Online Learning Journal 2026](https://olj.onlinelearningconsortium.org/index.php/olj/article/view/5188)
- Streaks: across multiple studies, highlighting a *broken* streak reduces later engagement relative to an intact one, even when the break was caused by events outside the person's control. The effect is stronger when people blame themselves and weaker when the streak can be "repaired." One author says people "just drop off once their streaks are broken." **[MODERATE–STRONG, multi-study JCR paper]** — [Silverman & Barasch 2023, J. Consumer Research (CU Boulder)](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [UDel repository](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb); [CU news](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)

**Civic platforms specifically**
- Waze: reporting is described as driven by reciprocity ("pay-it-forward", because others' reports helped me) plus points, badges and a leaderboard. There are baseline rewards for passive use and incremental rewards for active reporting. **[WEAK; business-school case study, not peer-reviewed]** — [Harvard D3 case](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/waze-leveraging-the-crowd-to-find-your-way/index.html)
- Colab (Brazil): points for every completed activity or message, plus quizzes on how government works. The founder says the aim is to identify who participates most. **[WEAK; press/interview]** — [Exame interview](https://exame.com/tecnologia/criador-do-colab-explica-sua-rede-social-para-a-cidadania/); [Conexão TO (Palmas)](https://www.conexaoto.com.br/2021/10/22/rede-social-da-prefeitura-de-palmas-colab-ganha-adesao-da-populacao). Scale in 2020: >100 municipalities and >300k users (company figures). — [Startupi](https://startupi.com.br/2020/07/startup-que-leva-inovacao-a-governos-capta-mais-de-r-3-milhoes-em-novo-investimento/). Saquarema (RJ), one year: 6,902 downloads, 1,392 zeladoria requests, 68.7% attended. The download-to-report ratio shows a big install-to-contribute drop. **[WEAK, municipal self-report]** — [Diário Oficial de Saquarema](https://dos.saquarema.rj.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/D.O.S._1642-7_assinado.pdf)

### Inferences
- Plan around a power-law population. Most users will file exactly one emergency or sighting. A core of perhaps hundreds to low thousands of protetores and ONG staff per metro will do most of the chain-building. These are two different products: (a) a near-zero-friction "one-shot" reporter flow and (b) a professional-grade case tool for the core. Optimizing only for "engagement" metrics of the median user is a trap.
- The second contribution is the conversion event that matters. Sauermann's 27% average return rate and iNaturalist's 15–20% year-over-year retention give realistic baselines. A platform that reaches 25–30% second-report rates would be performing at the citizen-science norm (inference from cited baselines).
- Self-determination theory (SDT; Deci & Ryan, background theory not re-verified here) frames the levers: *autonomy* (I chose to help), *competence* (my report was useful and identifiable), *relatedness* (a community and the animal). The evidence above is consistent with it. Informational, symbolic recognition works (Gallus, Restivo); controlling, contingent rewards tilt toward quantity over quality (Cerasoli).
- In this domain, quantity-incentives are actively harmful. Points per report would reward duplicate sightings, low-quality photos, and spammy chain extensions, which degrade the future re-ID pipeline.
- Streaks fit badly because emergencies are not a daily habit. Any streak mechanic will break for nearly everyone, and broken streaks cause drop-off.
- Newcomer rejection, as in Wikipedia, is the main socialization risk. Veteran protetores correcting or dismissing newcomers' reports ("isso já foi resolvido", "foto inútil") in a chain chat is the equivalent of Wikipedia reverts.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of Waze reporter motivation or retention was found; only case-study and vendor material.
- No Ushahidi or SeeClickFix participation-inequality or retention statistics were retrieved in this session.
- No independent academic evaluation of Colab's gamification effect on participation was found.
- The full results of Jackson/Østerlund on the role of "Talk" forums in Zooniverse retention were not retrieved, so the effect of discussion on retention is unverified.
- No experiment isolating whether fast feedback on a newcomer's *first* iNaturalist observation raises retention was found, even though it is widely assumed.

## 2. Prosocial behavior toward animals: identifiable victim, empathy, distance, compassion fatigue, moral licensing, bystander effect, and countermeasures

### Takeaway
The "classic" persuasion effects are weaker than folklore suggests. The identifiable-victim effect and moral licensing both shrink toward zero after correcting for publication bias. The bystander effect is real in lab meta-analyses, but in real public emergencies someone usually helps. The best-evidenced lever for turning bystanders into helpers is **proximity-targeted, specific, personal dispatch**: mobile alerts to nearby volunteers raised bystander CPR from 48% to 62% in an RCT. Expect a steep funnel (≈38% accept, ≈14% actually help). The care burden falls on a few protetores, and burnout is driven by intake load and lack of support.

### Cited Findings

**Identifiable victim effect (IVE) & compassion fade**
- Meta-analysis of 41 studies: the IVE is significant but modest (r = .05). The three highest-powered studies show effects of almost zero. The effect is most reliable when comparing a single identified victim with a single unidentified one. **[CONTESTED]** — [Lee & Feeley 2016, Social Influence (SKKU)](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/the-identifiable-victim-effect-a-meta-analytic-review/)
- A reanalysis found moderate evidence of publication bias and "strong evidence for the absence" of an IVE (model-averaged r = 0.002). A 2024 preregistered replication of Small et al. (2007) found no support for the IVE. **[CONTESTED; secondary summary]** — [Wikipedia: Identifiable victim effect (citing Maier et al. 2023; Collabra 2024)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identifiable_victim_effect)
- Compassion fade also occurs when the victims are non-human animals, but only among non-environmentalists. **[MODERATE]** — [Markowitz, Slovic, Västfjäll & Hodges 2013, JDM (PDF)](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/21D72101A0ACD52E4737642102335EEE/S193029750000526Xa.pdf/compassion-fade-and-the-challenge-of-environmental-conservation.pdf). In related work, decline in compassion "may begin with the second endangered life." **[MODERATE]** — [Västfjäll et al. 2014, PLOS ONE (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4062481/). The authors suggest framing victims as one cohesive group (e.g., "save a species"). — [Columbia State of the Planet](https://news.climate.columbia.edu/2013/09/20/a-tip-of-the-psychological-scales-and-what-it-means-for-conservation-efforts/)

**Emotion in animal appeals**
- Dog-photo donation experiments (HSE, Russia): sadness and anger increased donations and happiness decreased them. Photos of healthy domestic dogs were the *least* effective, and the expectation that puppies would draw the most did not hold. Arousal did not correlate with willingness to donate. **[WEAK; n≈45–54, simulated donation]** — [HSE news](https://www.hse.ru/en/news/research/1000014891.html); [Heliyon paper PDF](https://hse.ru/data/2024/08/06/1896645324/1-s2.0-S2405844023109364-main.pdf)
- A study in the International Journal of Advertising (summarized) ranks one sad or distressed single victim's face as the most persuasive donation visual. Happy victims are generally ineffective at raising money, but happy *groups* raise perceived charity credibility. **[WEAK; secondary summary]** — [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/tender-paws/202401/5-ways-animal-rescues-can-increase-charitable-donations)

**Bystander effect / diffusion of responsibility**
- Meta-analysis (1960s–2010; 105 effect sizes, >7,700 participants): bystander presence reduces helping overall (g = −0.35), but effects are non-negative in some *dangerous* emergencies. **[STRONG]** — [Fischer et al. 2011, Psychological Bulletin (Regensburg)](https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/20599/)
- Real-world CCTV of 219 public conflicts in the UK, the Netherlands and South Africa: in about 9 of 10 conflicts at least one bystander, typically several, intervened. More bystanders were associated with a *higher* likelihood that someone helped. **[STRONG, observational]** — [Philpot et al. 2020, American Psychologist (PDF)](https://gwern.net/doc/crime/2020-philpot.pdf); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/cross-national-cctv-footage-reveals-that-bystanders-intervene-in-9-out-of-10-public-conflicts/)
- Online: earlier reviews report diffusion of responsibility on message boards, and "virtual" others reduce helping in email, chat and forums. However, a Facebook experiment sending help requests to 1, 3, 6 or 9 friends (n = 176) found that **personalized greetings clearly increased responses**, with no support for diffusion by group size. **[WEAK–MODERATE; single study]** — [Fatkin & Lansdown, Information Technology & People](https://www.emerald.com/itp/article/30/1/71/176758/Group-size-and-personalization-s-effect-on); [Heriot-Watt record](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/group-size-and-personalizations-effect-on-facebook-message-respon/)

**Countermeasure with the strongest evidence: proximity-targeted dispatch of volunteers (analog domain: cardiac arrest)**
- RCT in Stockholm (2012–2013): an SMS/phone system located CPR-trained volunteers within 500 m of a suspected cardiac arrest and dispatched them. Bystander CPR was **62% (188/305) vs 48% (172/360)**, an absolute +14 pp (95% CI 6–21; p < 0.001). 9,828 volunteers were recruited, with no major adverse events. There was no significant survival difference in this trial. **[STRONG]** — [Ringh et al. 2015, NEJM (ACC journal scan)](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2015/06/10/16/09/Mobile-Phone-Dispatch-of-Laypersons-for-CPR-in-OHCA); [SGEM review](https://thesgem.com/2016/01/sgem143-call-me-maybe-for-bystander-cpr/)
- Responder funnel (Australia/New Zealand): of 1,985 alerted volunteers, 75.6% saw the alert, 37.7% accepted, 27.1% arrived, and 14.3% provided care. Prior experience was associated with higher odds of giving care. **[MODERATE]** — [Monash binational survey](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-binational-survey-of-smartphone-activated-volunteer-responders-/)
- Reasons responders declined alerts (community first-responder app, 3 years): the mission was no longer available, they were at work, the situation was inappropriate, they were caring for minors, they felt unwell, or they feared making a mistake. **[MODERATE]** — [BMC Cardiovascular Disorders 2025, table](https://biomedcentral-bmccardiovascdisord.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/articles/10.1186/s12872-025-04586-y/tables/2)
- UK GoodSAM registry: when an alerted responder attended, survival roughly doubled. **[MODERATE, observational]** — [NIHR Evidence alert](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/more-people-survived-cardiac-arrest-first-aiders-goodsam-alert/)

**Proximity matters for lost pets too**
- US survey: about 14% of dogs and 15% of cats were lost in the past 5 years. 93% of dogs and 75% of cats were recovered. Dogs were most often found by searching the neighborhood or came home on their own, and 14% were found via an ID tag. Cats most often returned on their own and were less likely to carry ID. **[MODERATE; US data]** — [Weiss, Slater & Lord 2012, Animals (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4494319/)

**Compassion fatigue & burnout among rescuers**
- Michigan shelter volunteers: the biggest stressor was shelters with *open-intake* policies. Euthanasia itself was not associated with compassion fatigue in volunteers. **[MODERATE]** — [Faunalytics summary](https://faunalytics.org/compassion-fatigue-in-animal-shelter-volunteers/)
- Georgia (US) rescue volunteers (259 responses, 104 organizations): perceived organizational support correlated negatively with burnout (τb = −.230) and positively with compassion satisfaction (τb = .323), but not with secondary traumatic stress. **[WEAK; undergraduate symposium]** — [Kennesaw State](https://digitalcommons.kennesaw.edu/undergradsymposiumksu/spring2025/spring2025/254/)
- Shelter and rescue workers and volunteers (n = 143, mixed methods, 2025): support groups were the most preferred mental-health intervention. **[WEAK–MODERATE]** — [Human-Animal Interactions 2025 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/03ed03f2eb0f4168b572e9551f16bb59)
- Canadian animal rescue workers: depression, compassion fatigue and trauma scores were significantly inter-correlated. **[WEAK]** — [MacEwan University](https://roam.macewan.ca/signposting/describedby/42faf06d-2e8e-4170-ac73-cc48c6a551be)
- Brazil, protetores independentes in Alagoa Grande (PB): most earned up to 2 minimum wages and had completed secondary school. They assisted about 18 animals each on average, and the main difficulty they cited was lack of municipal support. **[WEAK; small local TCC]** — [UFPB repository](https://repositorio.ufpb.br/jspui/handle/123456789/23598)
- Brazil, Rio de Janeiro protetores (ethnography): recruitment into protection comes through veterinary courses and sociability networks, plus *self-recruitment via the internet, especially Facebook*. — [Osório 2016, Vibrant (SciELO)](https://www.scielo.br/j/vb/a/pQ5bpKbhCWCT6bv8cGkzchC/?lang=en). Activity is financed by mutual-support networks of online supporters, part of the pet industry (including vet clinics), and some municipalities. Osório interprets protetores as tending to reject professionalization because they operate on "gift" (dádiva) relations. **[MODERATE, qualitative]** — [Osório 2017, Ambivalências](https://periodicos.ufs.br/Ambivalencias/article/download/7508/6767/24184)

**Moral licensing**
- A meta-analysis of 91 studies (7,397 participants) estimated d = 0.31, but published studies showed larger effects than unpublished ones. — [Blanken, van de Ven & Zeelenberg 2015, PSPB](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167215572134). Three well-powered replications of Sachdeva et al. (2009), including an MTurk study with N = 940, failed. — [Blanken et al. 2014 (Tilburg)](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/three-attempts-to-replicate-the-moral-licensing-effect/). Bias-corrected estimates range from d = −0.05 (PET-PEESE) to d = 0.18 (3PSM), and the evidence "has likely been inflated by publication bias." **[CONTESTED]** — [Kuper & Bott 2019, Meta-Psychology](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/878)

### Inferences
- **Do not build the product on the IVE.** Each chain naturally centers on one identifiable animal, which costs nothing, but the large donation effects claimed for single identified victims are not reliable. The more defensible pattern is to avoid presenting many victims at once (compassion fade starts at the second victim) and to frame aggregates as one cohesive group ("os gatos da praça X").
- **Proximity plus specific personal ask is the core anti-bystander mechanism.** Translate the CPR-dispatch model: when an emergency is posted, notify a small set of nearby, opted-in helpers with a specific task ("Você está a ~400 m. Pode confirmar se o cão ainda está lá e enviar uma foto?"). Generic broadcasts ("alguém ajuda!") are the weak alternative.
- **Size alerts using the funnel.** The Monash ratio (1,985 alerted to 283 who provided care) implies roughly 7 alerts per helper who actually acts in a time-critical setting. For stray-animal emergencies, where helpers need transport or money and the stakes feel lower, the ratio is likely worse. Escalate by expanding radius and recipients over time rather than blasting everyone at once (inference; requires local A/B data).
- **Expose "someone is already on it" as visible commitment.** A "Estou indo" or "Eu assumo" button with an ETA does two things. It counters the "someone else will help" assumption by making the actual someone visible, and it removes the top decline reason ("mission no longer available") by de-alerting others when a case is covered. Main failure mode: claim-and-abandon, where someone claims a case and doesn't follow through, so a claim needs a timeout plus a check-in prompt.
- **For lost pets, sightings near the loss point are the most valuable.** Neighborhood search and self-return dominate recovery, so cats warrant smaller alert radii (inference).
- **Burnout is a platform-design problem, not only a personal one.** The platform risks becoming an "open-intake" funnel that routes every emergency to the same few protetores, which is exactly the top stressor for shelter volunteers. Caseload caps, pause mode, handoffs, and visible organizational or peer support are design levers.
- **Moral licensing is too weak and contested to justify design decisions.** The practical analog worth guarding against is "reporting = done." Every report confirmation should present the next concrete action and who else has been notified.

### Gaps
- Results of Levin, Arluke & Irvine (2017), "Are People More Disturbed by Dog or Human Suffering?" (Society & Animals), could not be verified. The full text is closed-access, and the bibliographic record is at [CU Experts](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_155308).
- No Brazilian study measuring burnout or compassion fatigue specifically among protetores independentes was found.
- No study directly tests proximity-targeted alerts for *animal* emergencies. The CPR literature is an analog with different stakes and training levels.
- The result of the 2024 JDM replication of Kogut & Ritov's singularity effect was not retrieved: [Cambridge JDM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/3DA967642DBCD4E6117CCA104F3D6854/core-reader).
- No evidence was found on psychological distance (construal level) for animal helping specifically.

## 3. Behavioral economics of reporting friction: effort, defaults, small asks, progressive disclosure, timing, notification fatigue

### Takeaway
Perceived length and effort up front predict who starts and who finishes, so make the first ask tiny and the stated effort explicit. Defaults are among the more robust nudges but are highly heterogeneous. The broader nudge literature is inflated by publication bias: real-world nudge-unit effects are about one-sixth of published ones, so treat any nudge as a hypothesis to A/B test. Foot-in-the-door is a weak effect. Notification load is already high (≈56–64 per day). Batching non-urgent alerts improves well-being, while turning everything off raises anxiety and FoMO.

### Cited Findings
- **Stated length:** the longer the *stated* survey length, the fewer respondents start and finish. The announced length on the welcome screen was the strongest predictor of breakoff. **[MODERATE]** — [Statistics Canada (citing Galesic & Bosnjak 2009)](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/2013002/article/11885/section4-eng.htm)
- **Length vs breakoff:** a 30-minute survey had about 40% higher breakoff risk than a 10-minute one, and a 20-minute survey about 20% higher. **[MODERATE; secondary citation of Galesic 2006]** — [Statistics in Transition 2021 (PDF)](https://cejsh.icm.edu.pl/cejsh/element/bwmeta1.element.ojs-doi-10_21307_stattrans-2021-015/c/12-3f8419bd-0a6a-443f-9ee6-290861a793ea.pdf.pdf). A ~50-minute web survey had a final breakoff of 17.23%. Counter-evidence: one study (Peytchev 2009) found the cumulative number of questions had no effect on breakoff. — [Survey Practice](https://www.surveypractice.org/article/84347-breakoffs-in-an-hour-long-online-survey)
- **Chunking:** split work into small pieces that need little time or effort, and give feedback per contribution. — [Eveleigh et al. 2014, CHI](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1418573)
- **Defaults:** meta-analysis of 58 studies (n = 73,675) found an average shift of about d = 0.63–0.68, with huge heterogeneity (effects range from −0.5 to 2; I² ≈ 98% in a related analysis). Moderators explain little of the variance. **[MODERATE–STRONG, but heterogeneous]** — [Jachimowicz et al. 2019, Behavioural Public Policy (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/bpubpo/v3y2019i02p159-186_00.html); [Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/defaults-are-not-the-same-by-default/)
- **Nudge replication problem:** after correcting the Mertens et al. nudge meta-analysis for publication bias, "no evidence for nudge effectiveness" remains on average, although heterogeneity suggests some categories still work. — [Maier et al. 2022, PNAS (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9351501/). A later commentary notes the average is small but not exactly zero (d ≈ 0.04), with substantial heterogeneity. — [Replication Index 2026](https://replicationindex.com/2026/09/07/what-is-the-effect-of-nudging-43/). US nudge-unit trials, with all results visible, are statistically significant but about one-sixth the size of effects in published academic trials, mostly because of publication bias. **[STRONG]** — [Verfassungsblog on DellaVigna & Linos](https://verfassungsblog.de/nudging-after-the-replication-crisis/)
- **Foot-in-the-door (FITD) [CLASSIC, WEAK]:** a meta-analysis of 120 experimental groups found the effect replicable but weak, and nearly half the studies showed no effect or one in the wrong direction. — [Beaman et al. 1983, PSPB](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167283092002); review of the conditions under which it works: [Burger 1999, PSPR](https://cbsm.com/articles/32623-the-foot-in-the-door-compliance-procedure-a-multiple-process-analysis-and-review)
- **Notification load:** 15 users averaged 63.5 notifications per day, mostly from messengers and email, typically viewed within minutes even on silent. More notifications were associated with more negative emotions. — [Pielot et al. 2014, MobileHCI (PDF)](https://ic.unicamp.br/~oliveira/doc/MHCI2014_An-in-situ-study-of-mobile-phone-notifications.pdf). A larger sample (278 users, 794,525 notifications) found a median of 56 per day. **[MODERATE]** — [Pielot et al. 2018 (PDF)](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Pielot-MobileHCI18.pdf)
- **Batching RCT (n = 237):** batching notifications 3 times a day improved attention, productivity, mood, and sense of control, and lowered stress. Hourly batching barely differed from control. *No notifications at all* produced higher anxiety and FoMO. **[MODERATE–STRONG, RCT]** — [Fitz et al. 2019, Computers in Human Behavior (Springer Nature community)](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/batching-notifications-can-improve-attention-well-being-and-productivity)
- **Duplicate-report handling (FixMyStreet):** in a trial, the system checks for open reports in the same category nearby and offers to *subscribe to the existing report* instead of filing a duplicate. **[WEAK, practitioner]** — [mySociety blog 2019](https://www.mysociety.org/2019/04/24/stop-right-there-thats-already-been-reported/)
- **Install-to-contribute drop:** Saquarema's Colab saw 6,902 downloads against 1,392 requests in a year. **[WEAK]** — [D.O.S. Saquarema](https://dos.saquarema.rj.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/D.O.S._1642-7_assinado.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Emergency intake should be photo, then auto-location, then send.** Everything else (species, condition, exact vs approximate location, contact) should be optional, post-submission, or inferred (EXIF, ML). Show an explicit effort promise ("leva 10 segundos") because announced length is the strongest predictor of breakoff.
- **Account creation after the first report, not before.** Let people "claim" their report via phone or WhatsApp to receive updates. The feedback loop in §4 needs a contact channel, so the ask is "quer saber o que aconteceu com esse animal?", which is intrinsically motivating, not "crie uma conta."
- **Defaults to set:** location precision defaulting to *approximate* for public display, with exact location shared with responders (privacy-protective and consistent with the explicit exact-vs-approximate requirement). Follow-updates default ON for your own report. Nearby-alerts opt-in with a sensible default radius. Defaults are among the better-supported levers, but their effects vary wildly, so measure them.
- **Duplicates should become contributions.** When a nearby similar open chain exists, ask "É este animal? Adicionar sua foto ao caso." This turns a would-be duplicate into a chain-extension block, and it avoids the "rejected newcomer" dynamic.
- **Progressive "ladder of asks" (LPP-style):** report, then confirm still there, then add photo, then bring water or food, then transport, then foster or donate. FITD evidence is weak, so justify the ladder by reduced effort per step and legitimate peripheral participation, not by an assumed compliance boost.
- **Notification architecture:** (1) real-time push only for proximity emergencies and updates on your own cases; (2) everything else batched into a digest of at most 1–3 per day; (3) a user-set radius, quiet hours, and a per-chain mute. Never default to "off for everything," since the Fitz result suggests users then feel anxious about missing out.
- **Treat every behavioral nudge as a hypothesis to test.** Default expected effect sizes are small (one-sixth of published). Budget for A/B infrastructure early.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found on *form field count vs completion* for mobile civic reports specifically. Survey breakoff literature is the closest analog. Industry figures (e.g., HubSpot/Baymard) were not retrieved and would be vendor-grade evidence anyway.
- No data found on time-to-submit for FixMyStreet, SeeClickFix, or Colab reports.
- No study found on the optimal *timing* of reminder nudges in civic reporting.

## 4. Closing the loop: does feedback increase repeat reporting?

### Takeaway
Yes, and this is the best-evidenced lever in the whole brief. On FixMyStreet, a successful first experience was associated with a **57% higher probability of a second report** (n = 399,364 users). In Boston, residents who received **photos of the city fixing their requests submitted 60% more requests** over 13 months. Showing the work, including an honest backlog, builds trust. The *first* report's outcome is disproportionately predictive.

### Cited Findings
- FixMyStreet (n = 399,364 users): "A successful first experience using Fix My Street is associated with a 57 percent increase in the probability of an individual submitting a second report." Responsiveness to the first report "has predictive power over all future report submissions." The authors model this as "objective efficacy" in a calculus of participation. **[STRONG for association; observational, not randomized]** — [Sjoberg, Mellon & Peixoto 2017, Public Administration Review (ORA Oxford)](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2bd169fd-0b27-46e4-8b81-1ccfccf7c55a); [OIDP abstract](https://oidp.net/en/publication.php?id=1441); [Democracy Spot (author blog)](https://democracyspot.net/2017/03/)
- Boston (Citizens Connect / 311 app): residents who were sent **photos of the city addressing their own requests** went on to submit **60% more requests across 38% more categories** over the next 13 months. The boost lasted about 11 months after first exposure and was strongest among people who already found government fairly responsive. A website showing requests and the city's responses made residents 14% more trusting and 12% more supportive of government. Showing the growing *backlog* of unmet requests left trust no lower than showing nothing at all. **[STRONG; field data + experiments]** — [Buell, Porter & Norton 2021, MSOM (INFORMS)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/msom.2020.0877); [HBS working paper (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/14-034.html)
- Toronto 311 "Closing the Loop" tiers feedback by complexity: simple requests (≈80%) get confirmation and closure, medium (≈15%) get step-by-step milestones, and complex (≈5%) get in-progress notes and photos. **[WEAK; municipal plan]** — [City of Toronto 2026 background file](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/mmis/2026/se/bgrd/backgroundfile-286991.pdf)
- Councils report that missing feedback generates repeat reports. Northumberland cited over 9,000 reports with no closed loop under its previous system. **[WEAK; council self-report]** — [Northumberland County Council](https://northumberland.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s18551/03%20FMS%20AB%20LAC.pdf). Bath & NE Somerset reported improved automated responses and an SMS feedback option, alongside monthly reports rising from 638 (2022) to 875 (2023). This is correlational and confounded. — [B&NES FixMyStreet report](https://democracy.bathnes.gov.uk/documents/s78816/Fix%20My%20Street%20report.pdf)
- Citizen-science analog: scored feedback from scientists was "of great significance for the retention" of volunteers in marine research projects (Martin et al., cited). **[WEAK; secondary citation]** — [Aristeidou et al. 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815142)
- Brazil context: most Brazilians are skeptical that institutions adapt policies and services based on citizen feedback. **[MODERATE]** — [OECD 2023, Drivers of Trust in Public Institutions in Brazil](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-in-brazil_fb0e1896-en/full-report/component-5.html)
- Heavy civic-app users ask for the ability to track, re-open, and converse on existing requests. **[WEAK; single public submission]** — [ACT Legislative Assembly inquiry submission 2025](https://www.parliament.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/2911067/Submission-033-Ian-Diversi-18-July-2025_Redacted.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The "second report rate of first-time reporters" should be a North Star metric.** It is the measurable quantity that Sjoberg et al. tie to responsiveness.
- **Prioritize response to first-timers.** Route first-time reporters' emergencies to responders and moderators first, and badge the chain "primeiro relato" so veterans respond kindly. This mirrors iNaturalist's practice of filtering new users' observations.
- **Close the loop with photos of the outcome, not just status text.** Examples: animal at the vet, in a lar temporário, adopted, or a reunion with its tutor. The Buell result is specifically about *seeing the work*. Chains with timestamped data blocks already supply the raw material, so automatically push the latest photo block to everyone who contributed to that chain.
- **Be honest about unresolved cases.** Backlog transparency did not reduce trust. Showing "ainda sem ajuda há 6 h — 3 pessoas notificadas" is better than silence, and it doubles as an implicit ask.
- **Tier feedback like Toronto.** Sightings get a simple "obrigado, adicionado ao caso X." Emergencies get milestones. Long chains get narrative updates.
- **"Your sighting helped"** should be attributable. When a lost-pet chain is resolved, tell every sighting contributor that their block was part of the trail. This is a symbolic, informational recognition, consistent with Gallus and Restivo.
- In Brazil, where trust in institutions is low, the platform rather than the prefeitura may need to be the reliable closer of loops. It should not promise municipal action it cannot guarantee.

### Gaps
- The share of FixMyStreet users who report only once, and the exact operational definition of a "successful first experience" (marked fixed vs. acknowledged), were not retrievable (the ORA full text returned 403).
- No randomized test of loop-closing feedback was found in animal-welfare or lost-pet platforms.
- No academic study of 311/Toronto loop-closing on future reporting was found. Toronto's program is a plan, not an evaluation.

## 5. Emotional content: graphic images, content warnings/blur, outrage dynamics

### Takeaway
Negative emotion such as sadness can increase giving, but the evidence for *graphic gore* in animal advocacy is thin and untested for helping behavior. Content warnings and blur screens **do not reduce distress**: they raise anticipatory anxiety, and most people uncover the image anyway. Moral-emotional language spreads about 20% further per word, so emergency chains are structurally prone to outrage cascades.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis of 12 studies: content and trigger warnings **increased anticipatory anxiety** and had a trivial effect on emotional response after viewing (d = 0.02, CI −0.05 to 0.10) and on avoidance (d = 0.06, CI −0.09 to 0.21). There was no effect on educational outcomes. **[MODERATE–STRONG]** — [Bridgland, Jones & Bellet 2023, Clinical Psychological Science (APS)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/2023-october-content-warnings-distress.html); [Flinders record](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-efficacy-of-trigger-warnings-content-warni/)
- Instagram-style sensitive-content (blur) screens: participants showed significant increases in state anxiety and negative affect *whether or not* they uncovered the image beneath. Most participants, including more vulnerable ones (e.g., with higher depression), uncovered the first screen they saw, and many repeatedly uncovered screens. **[MODERATE; online samples]** — [PsyPost on Flinders study](https://www.psypost.org/instagrams-content-warning-screens-may-increase-anxiety-study-finds/); [Flinders news](https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2023/02/02/warning-screens-fail-to-deter-instagram-users-from-seeing-graphic-content/)
- Content moderation: the interactive-blurring work reports that blurring that moderators can reveal on demand helps reduce exposure to harmful content while maintaining speed and accuracy. — [Das et al., "Fast, Accurate, and Healthier: Interactive Blurring Helps Moderators Reduce Exposure to Harmful Content," HCOMP](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/HCOMP/article/view/7461). This contrasts with a summarized finding that blurring or greyscaling did *not* reduce negative effects of viewing disturbing images for simulated moderators, possibly because viewers mentally fill in the details. **[CONTESTED; the attribution of the null result to this specific paper is per a search summary]** — [Psychological Research 2026](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00426-026-02241-5)
- Animal-advocacy imagery: Faunalytics says existing research "is not enough to make strong claims," and that little work directly compares graphic material shown with versus without consent. A planned study would compare happy, sad, and graphic imagery for donations. **[WEAK; evidence gap acknowledged by the leading animal-advocacy research org]** — [Faunalytics research priorities](https://faunalytics.org/faunalytics-research-priorities-for-2020-and-beyond/)
- Sad and angry dog images increased donations relative to happy ones. Healthy dogs drew the least. **[WEAK]** — [HSE](https://www.hse.ru/en/news/research/1000014891.html)
- Outrage diffusion: across 563,312 tweets, each moral-emotional word increased retweet diffusion by about 20%. **[STRONG for political Twitter; domain transfer is an inference]** — [Brady et al. 2017, PNAS (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514704)
- Donor trust is fragile: 49% of Brazilian donors say they have stopped donating after negative news about organizations. **[MODERATE]** — [Monitor Mercantil on Pesquisa Doação Brasil 2024](https://monitormercantil.com.br/brasileira-estara-na-lista-time100-philanthropy-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Do not count on blur or warnings to protect users' wellbeing.** They don't reduce distress and they add anticipatory anxiety. Use blur for *consent and context*: keep gore off the public map and feed by default, and show it on tap to responders who need it for triage. This is an inference from cited findings and is untested for animal emergencies.
- **Separate the "identification photo" from the "injury photo."** Lead public views with a face or full-body photo of the animal and a factual status line. That supports helping, which a sad single face may do, and future re-ID. Keep wound close-ups as a responder-level attachment.
- **Outrage risk in per-chain chats.** Emergency chats invite moral-emotional language: blame on tutores, prefeitura, or CCZ, and accusations of abuse. That spreads and can escalate to doxxing and vigilantism. Structured update blocks (status, location, needs) should be first-class and free chat secondary, with rate limits, rules against identifying alleged abusers, and a "report to authority" path for maus-tratos (legal specifics are out of scope here and belong to the legal research track).
- **Shareability is a double-edged sword.** Outrage drives reach, and reach drives donations, which Pix campaigns depend on. But outrage-optimized sharing also invites fraud and burnout, and 49% of donors stop after negative news. Make the shareable artifact action-oriented ("precisa de transporte até 18h") rather than outrage-oriented.

### Gaps
- No experiment found on graphic photos of *injured stray animals* and willingness to physically help (vs. donate). This is a key evidence gap for this platform.
- No study found on compassion fatigue from *repeated exposure to emergency feeds* in animal-rescue groups (the WhatsApp or Facebook group "feed of suffering").
- Faunalytics' planned imagery-comparison study had no published results in what was retrieved.

## 6. Brazil-specific evidence: volunteering, WhatsApp mobilization, trust, donations (Pix "vaquinhas"), animal protection

### Takeaway
Brazilian giving and volunteering are broad but **emergency-driven and low-trust**. Formal volunteering is low by IBGE's strict measure (4.2%) but far higher by self-report (IDIS: 34% active). The animal cause is growing fast among volunteers (1% to 9% of volunteers, 2011–2021). Trust in local government is low (≈20%), and most people doubt institutions act on feedback. Mobilization happens on Facebook and WhatsApp, and WhatsApp is near-universal (97% use it daily). Crowdfunding (Vakinha) is massive, and peaks during disasters.

### Cited Findings

**Volunteering**

This may be a reporting error or a definitional change, so verify against IPB primary data before use.]**

**Brazilian civic-reporting precedent**
- Colab: points-based gamification, >100 municipalities, a resolution rate (68.7%) reported in one municipality. **[WEAK]** — see §1 sources.

### Inferences
- **The WhatsApp agent is not a "later" nice-to-have. It is probably the primary intake channel for the median Brazilian reporter**, given near-universal daily use and an app install-to-report drop like Colab's. Make chain links render well when shared into WhatsApp groups (OpenGraph preview with photo, status, and neighborhood), because that is where protetor networks already coordinate.
- **Design for surge events.** Brazilian giving is emergency-driven (50% donated to emergencies; RS floods). Zooniverse activity spikes with media, and newcomer retention after events is around 15–20%. Plan surge-mode onboarding with simplified intake, triage queues, and a welcome flow aimed at converting a fraction of event newcomers.
- **Trust is the scarce resource.** Low institutional trust, "49% stop after negative news," and gift-economy protetores who resist professionalization together imply that verification should be light-touch and reputation-based (community-vouched protetores, ONG CNPJ verification) rather than bureaucratic credentialing. Pix vaquinhas linked to a chain should show the case's need, spend, and outcome photos.
- **Demographic targeting.** Volunteers skew female, 50+, and higher-education (IBGE). Large tap targets, readable fonts, and WhatsApp-first flows serve the likely core contributors. Youth are the least likely volunteers, so don't design the core experience around them.
- **The animal cause is growing as a volunteer focus (1% to 9%).** There is a growing pool to recruit, but existing protetores already carry a heavy load (§2), so expanding the *casual* helper layer (transport, temporary feeding, sightings) is where new capacity should come from.

### Gaps
- The share of Pesquisa Doação Brasil 2024 donors who give to the animal cause could not be read unambiguously from the infographic.
- No Brazilian academic study of WhatsApp groups used for animal rescue or lost pets was found. The closest is Facebook-focused ethnography (Osório).
- No published count or profile of protetores independentes nationally was found, beyond small local studies.
- No 2024+ OECD trust wave for Brazil was found; the latest is the 2022 fieldwork.
- No data found on Pix vaquinha fraud rates or on the animal-category share of Vakinha campaigns.

## 7. Design implications for the platform (each tied to evidence) and anti-patterns

### Takeaway
The highest-confidence levers are: (1) a near-zero-friction first report, (2) visible, photo-based loop-closing, especially for first-time reporters, (3) proximity-targeted, specific, personal asks with visible commitment, (4) symbolic, informational, peer-given recognition rather than points or leaderboards, and (5) protecting the small core of protetores from intake overload. Everything nudge-like should be A/B tested, because published effect sizes are inflated.

### Cited Findings (evidence anchors; details in §§1–6)
- Most contributors do one thing once, and about 10% do about 80%. — [Sauermann & Franzoni 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4311847); [JR summary](https://journalistsresource.org/?p=44784)
- A successful first experience is associated with a 57% higher probability of a second report. — [Sjoberg, Mellon & Peixoto 2017](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2bd169fd-0b27-46e4-8b81-1ccfccf7c55a)
- Photos of the work produced 60% more requests over 13 months, and backlog transparency did not hurt trust. — [Buell, Porter & Norton 2021](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/msom.2020.0877)
- Proximity dispatch raised bystander action from 48% to 62%. The funnel is 38% accept and 14% act. — [Ringh 2015](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2015/06/10/16/09/Mobile-Phone-Dispatch-of-Laypersons-for-CPR-in-OHCA); [Monash](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-binational-survey-of-smartphone-activated-volunteer-responders-/)
- Symbolic awards raised newcomer retention for about a year, and barnstars raised productivity by about 60%. — [Gallus 2017](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2540); [Restivo & van de Rijt 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315525)
- Badges, points, and leaderboards show no significant learning effect, and only about 20% of users respond to badges. — [ISCTE meta-analysis](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/29213/1/article_95533.pdf); [Hoernle et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.06160)
- Broken streaks cause drop-off. — [Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)
- Contingent incentives favor quantity over quality. — [Cerasoli et al. 2014](https://oa.mg/work/10.1037/a0035661)
- Newcomer rejection drives community decline. — [Halfaker et al. 2013](https://stuartgeiger.com/papers/abs-rise-and-decline-wikipedia.pdf)
- Blur and warnings don't reduce distress. — [Bridgland et al. 2023](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/2023-october-content-warnings-distress.html); [Flinders Instagram study](https://www.psypost.org/instagrams-content-warning-screens-may-increase-anxiety-study-finds/)
- Batching notifications 3 times a day improves well-being, while turning them all off raises FoMO. — [Fitz et al. 2019](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/batching-notifications-can-improve-attention-well-being-and-productivity)
- Open intake is the top volunteer stressor, and organizational support is linked to lower burnout. — [Faunalytics](https://faunalytics.org/compassion-fatigue-in-animal-shelter-volunteers/); [Kennesaw](https://digitalcommons.kennesaw.edu/undergradsymposiumksu/spring2025/spring2025/254/)
- Nudge-unit effects are about one-sixth of published effects. — [Verfassungsblog on DellaVigna & Linos](https://verfassungsblog.de/nudging-after-the-replication-crisis/)

### Inferences — concrete design implications

**A. Getting people to start (first contribution)**
1. **The first emergency report is photo, then auto-location, then send, with no login required.** Everything else is optional or post-hoc. The UI says "leva 10 segundos." *(Stated-length and breakoff evidence; dabbler majority.)*
2. **The WhatsApp intake agent is a first-class channel early in the roadmap,** not just a later add-on. *(97% daily WhatsApp use; Colab's download-to-report drop.)*
3. **Duplicates are routed into existing chains** ("É este animal? Adicione sua foto"), so a potential rejection becomes a contribution. *(mySociety duplicate trial; Halfaker newcomer-rejection.)*
4. **Location defaults to approximate in public and exact for verified responders,** with an explicit toggle. *(Defaults are powerful but heterogeneous, so measure; also privacy.)*

**B. Getting people to act (bystander to helper)**
5. **Proximity-targeted, named, specific asks:** notify a handful of opted-in nearby helpers with one concrete micro-task, then expand radius and recipients in waves if nobody accepts. *(Ringh RCT; Facebook personalization result.)*
6. **Visible commitment:** "Estou indo / Eu assumo" with an ETA, which auto-de-alerts others. A claim timeout with a check-in prevents claim-and-abandon. *(Main decline reason is "mission no longer available"; diffusion of responsibility.)*
7. **A ladder of micro-roles** (confirm, photograph, feed or water, transport, foster, donate) shown as "o que você pode fazer agora." *(LPP; chunking; FITD is weak, so justify by lower effort, not compliance.)*
8. **One animal at a time in asks;** avoid "37 animais precisam de ajuda perto de você" as the primary call to action. *(Compassion fade from the 2nd victim. IVE itself is contested, so this is low-cost hedging, not a core bet.)*

**C. Getting people to come back (retention)**
9. **Photo-based loop closing pushed to every contributor of a chain,** with explicit attribution ("sua foto de 14/10 ajudou a localizar o Thor"). *(Sjoberg +57%; Buell +60%.)*
10. **First-time reporters' cases are prioritized** in responder queues and flagged in the chain so veterans respond kindly. *(First-experience effect; iNaturalist newcomer practice; Wikipedia reverts.)*
11. **Honest status for unresolved cases** ("6 h sem ajuda, 3 pessoas notificadas") instead of silence. *(Backlog transparency did not reduce trust.)*
12. **Recognition is symbolic, peer-given, and specific:** thank-yous from the protetor who resolved the case, chain "agradecimentos," and a profile history of animals helped. There are no global points. *(Gallus; Restivo; Cerasoli quality vs quantity.)*
13. **Notifications:** real-time only for nearby emergencies and own-case updates; everything else in a digest of at most 1–3 per day; user-set radius, quiet hours, per-chain mute; never default all-off. *(Pielot 56–64/day; Fitz batching RCT.)*

**D. Protecting the core (protetores, ONGs, CCZ)**
14. **Caseload visibility, caps, handoff, and "pausa" mode without guilt messaging,** plus routing that spreads cases across helpers rather than pinging the same top protetores. *(Open-intake stressor; ~10% do ~80% of work, so losing them collapses the system.)*
15. **Peer-support affordances** such as optional protetor circles and a way to step back from a chain gracefully. *(Support groups are the preferred intervention; perceived organizational support is linked to lower burnout.)*
16. **Gentle moderation tools and response templates for veterans** so newcomer reports are corrected without being rejected. *(Halfaker.)*

**E. Content, emotion, and trust**
17. **Separate the ID photo (public) from the injury photo (responder tap-to-view).** Don't treat blur as a wellbeing feature; it is a consent and context feature. *(Bridgland; Flinders Instagram studies; moderator-blur evidence contested.)*
18. **Structured update blocks first, chat second;** moderation rules against identifying or harassing alleged abusers; a "denunciar maus-tratos às autoridades" path. *(+20% diffusion per moral-emotional word; outrage cascades.)*
19. **Donations (Pix) are tied to a chain's stated needs with spend and outcome photos;** light-touch verification for receivers (CNPJ for ONGs, community vouching for protetores). *(49% stop giving after negative news; emergency-driven giving; gift-economy protetores.)*
20. **Surge mode** for disasters and media spikes: simplified intake, triage, and a newcomer welcome flow. *(Zooniverse spikes; 50% donate to emergencies; RS 2024 floods.)*
21. **Instrument from day one:** first-to-second report conversion, alert-to-accept-to-arrive funnel, time-to-first-response for first-timers, and protetor caseload distribution (Gini). A/B test every nudge. *(Nudge effects ~1/6 of published; defaults heterogeneous.)*

### Inferences — anti-patterns to avoid
- **Global leaderboards and points per report.** These reward duplicates and low-quality photos, demotivate the bottom of the ranking, and show no significant effect in meta-analyses.
- **Streaks of any kind.** Emergencies aren't daily habits, and broken streaks cause drop-off.
- **Cash or material rewards per report.** These cause crowding toward quantity and invite fraud.
- **Mandatory sign-up or long forms before the first report.** Stated effort drives breakoff.
- **"Alguém ajuda!" broadcasts to everyone,** with no specific ask, no proximity, and no visible claimant.
- **Silent closure,** where reports vanish with no outcome feedback. This forfeits the 57% and 60% effects.
- **Feeds of gore or outrage-optimized sharing.** These drive avoidance, distress, outrage cascades, vigilantism, and donor distrust.
- **Treating warnings or blur as "safety solved."**
- **Funneling all cases to the top 1% of protetores,** which builds an "open-intake" burnout machine.
- **Letting veterans publicly shame or "revert" newcomers** in chain chats.
- **Promising municipal action the platform can't guarantee,** in a context where only about 20% trust local government.
- **"N animals near you" mass lists as the primary call to action** (compassion fade and overwhelm).

### Gaps
- Most implications are cross-domain transfers (citizen science, 311, CPR dispatch, Wikipedia). There are almost no controlled studies on *animal-emergency* platforms specifically, so every implication should be validated with in-platform experiments.
- The optimal alert radius and recipient count for stray-animal emergencies in Brazilian cities is unknown and must be learned empirically.
- Behavioral evidence is thin on the effectiveness of a chat-centric case model (per-chain chat) vs structured updates for sustaining participation. The Zooniverse "Talk" results were not retrieved, and FixMyStreet councils explicitly resist forum-style use ([Bromley](https://www.bromley.gov.uk/fixservices)).
